# Chapter I · The First Light

# Solara — Novice of the Dawn
*Dawnforged · Mystic · Tier I · Chapter I · The First Light*

> Status: PROPOSED lore (draft for Jerian's approval). Canon quotes are marked [canon].

## Who they are

"Her order believes the Crown is the first sunrise, stolen. She has come to return it." [canon]

Solara is the youngest voice of a very old faith. She arrives in the Crownfall with a simple brass staff, a travelling cloak that still smells of temple incense, and a certainty that most champions lost years ago. She does not want the Crown for herself. She wants to carry it home, the way you would carry back a lantern someone borrowed without asking.

## Before the Crownfall

Solara grew up in the Dawnforged realm, a land of high terraces where the sun is greeted every morning with bells. Her order keeps the Dawn Temple, a house of white stone built to face east.

The elders taught her that the world once had a first sunrise, brighter than any since, and that it was taken. They point to the Crown hanging over the Arena and say: there it is. Solara believed this the way children believe the sky is blue. Then she was chosen to go and see for herself.

## Why they answered the call

Her order sent her, and she went gladly. She answered the call of a Sovereign because a novice cannot claim the Crown alone. Privately, she has a second reason: she wants to know if the elders were right. She has never told anyone that she is not sure.

## The Ascension

It happened in her third bout, on the eastern edge of the Arena, where the light comes first. Her side was losing. A knight of her own banner had fallen, and Solara was kneeling over him with nothing left to give. The sun had not yet cleared the Arena wall.

She did not pray for victory. She prayed that the light would reach him in time.

The shards around the Crown turned, all at once, and a single beam fell across the sand. It struck her staff and the brass changed. It became a disc of gold, humming like a struck bell. Her plain robes ran with gilt thread, as if someone were stitching them in light. The fallen knight drew breath.

Solara stood up. The crowd had gone very quiet.

"The light answers me now." [canon]

She said it softly, almost as a question. Then she raised the sun-disc staff and every champion on her side was wrapped in a warm, bright shield. The cost was small but real: from that day, she could no longer tell where her own faith ended and the Crown's answer began.

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension

Months later, at the end of a long day, the Arena was nearly dark. Shadows stretched across the sand, and a rival Sovereign's champions pressed in from every side. Solara's friends were tired. She was tired too.

She remembered the bells of the Dawn Temple, the ones rung every morning whether anyone was listening or not.

So she began the morning hymn, in the evening.

Light gathered above her head: one sun, then a second, then a third, turning slowly in a halo. Her vestments became radiant robes, and from her shoulders opened wings of morning light, soft and pale gold. The shadows did not flee. They simply stopped being shadows.

Every champion on her banner stood a little taller, their guard firmer, their wounds closing. When the bout ended, she walked among them and touched each one on the shoulder.

"Rest. The sun rises." [canon]

She is still a novice in her order's eyes. She no longer minds.

## Voice and temperament

Solara is gentle, earnest and stubborn. She speaks in short, kind sentences and rarely raises her voice. She blesses people before battle without asking if they want it. Her greeting is "The dawn walks with you." [canon]

## Bonds and rivalries

- **Sol** — Solara's order has a hymn about the first light given a body. When she first saw Sol in the Arena, she dropped her staff.
- **Solenne Ash** — A fellow Dawnforged champion and the closest thing Solara has to an older sister in the Crownfall. Solara has asked to see Solenne's list. Solenne has refused.
- **Elara** — Two healers from different worlds. They work side by side well.

## Beneath the Arena (Set 2)

When the banners went down beneath the Arena, Solara found the first place she had ever been where the sun simply did not reach. It frightened her more than any rival. She lit a small dawn-lamp from her staff and found that it held.

Below, she became the **Lamp of the Lost**, a Restorer who walks the dark side-passages looking for explorers who took a wrong turn. Her ability, **Lamplight**, shields a friend when she arrives and, as she grows, mends what the depths have broken.

## In the campaign

Solara is the very first champion most players meet, and her card is in the starter deck.

- **Trial 1 · Bout 1 — "The First Morning":** A tutorial Bout. Solara guides the player through placing a champion and using Shield.
- **Trial 1 · Bout 3 — "Hold the Light":** A puzzle Bout. The player must keep Solara on the field for five turns while the rival only attacks her. Win by shielding her in time.
- **Trial 2 · Bout 2 — "The Novice's Test":** Solara tests the player with her own deck of Dawnforged healers and shields.

# Rowan — Longbow of the Realm
*Kingdom · Ranger · Tier I · Chapter I · The First Light*

> Status: PROPOSED lore (draft for Jerian's approval). Canon quotes are marked [canon].

## Who they are

"Rowan has never missed at a hundred paces. At a thousand, he says, the wind argues." [canon]

Rowan is a kingdom archer with a yew longbow and a calm, easy smile. He is the kind of person who talks to the weather as if it were an old friend who sometimes owes him money.

He is not chasing glory. He likes the Arena because it is the widest open space he has ever seen, and he likes the Crown because it is the farthest target he has ever had.

## Before the Crownfall

Rowan grew up in the Kingdom realm, on the edge of the great royal forest where the king's foresters keep the deer and the roads safe. His grandfather taught him to shoot with a bow taller than he was. By sixteen he was a royal forester himself, wearing the green tabard and walking the long forest roads.

Life was quiet and good. He guarded travellers, chased off wolves, and won the summer archery fair so often they asked him, politely, to stop entering.

## Why they answered the call

The Kingdom sent champions to the Crownfall, and Rowan was not on the list. He went anyway, as an escort for those who were.

He answered a Sovereign's call partly out of duty to the realm and partly out of plain curiosity. He wants to know how far he can really shoot, and what the wind says up there.

## The Ascension

It happened on a bright, gusty afternoon. Rowan's banner was pinned down, and the rival's strongest champion stood at the far end of the Arena, well beyond any normal range. Everyone told him the shot was impossible. The wind was howling across the sand.

Rowan licked a finger, held it up, and listened.

For the first time in his life, the wind went quiet. It was not still; it was waiting.

His plain kit shimmered and became a royal green tabard, crisp as new leaves. His yew bow grew silver limbs that caught the light like moonlit water. He drew, held, and smiled.

"A thousand paces, now." [canon]

The arrow flew straight across the whole Arena and struck true. The rival champion fell. The crowd was silent for a moment, then loud. Rowan only nodded and thanked the wind under his breath. He did not know whether the Crown had helped him or simply let him be what he already was. He has decided not to mind.

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension

Later in the season, Rowan's Sovereign faced a rival with flying champions, high above the reach of any ordinary archer. Rowan was out of arrows, his quiver empty, his friends below him holding a shrinking line.

A falcon landed on his shoulder. He had never seen it before. It looked at him as if it had known him for years.

He drew his empty bow, and an arrow of light formed on the string. A cloak of gold settled around his shoulders, heavy and warm, shaped like a lion's mane. The falcon screamed and took off ahead of the shot, showing him the way.

Arrow after arrow of light rose into the sky, each one finding its mark. When the last flyer fell, Rowan lowered his bow. The falcon came back to his arm and stayed.

"Clean shots." [canon]

He named the falcon Breeze. He says she argues less than the wind.

## Voice and temperament

Rowan is relaxed, friendly and dryly funny. He speaks slowly and never wastes words. His greeting is "The wind is kind today." [canon] The one thing he does not joke about is a shot taken carelessly.

## Bonds and rivalries

- **Kael** — Rowan and Kael are both humble Kingdom champions, and they became friends in their first week. Rowan thinks Kael takes everything too seriously. He follows him anyway, because someone should watch his back.
- **General Varr** — Varr wants archers in rows. Rowan prefers to wander off and find a better angle. They argue every bout. Varr has never once said Rowan was wrong afterwards.
- **Kira Volt** — An arc-tech gunner who rebuilt her first pistol from a lightning rod. Rowan finds her pistol fascinating and slightly unfair. They have a standing contest about who can hit the farthest target.

## Beneath the Arena (Set 2)

Beneath the Arena, there is no wind. Rowan felt it the moment the banners went down the great stair. The air was still and old, and for the first time his arrows had nothing to talk to.

He stayed anyway. Below, he became the **Archer of the Deep Gate**, a Sharpshooter who holds the great gate to the under-halls with his bow drawn into the dark. His ability, **Gatewatch Volley**, sends steady arrows into whatever comes through, every time he attacks.

He has started talking to the silence instead. He swears it is starting to answer.

## In the campaign

- **Trial 1 · Bout 5 — "A Hundred Paces":** A tutorial Bout teaching Reach. The rival plays flying champions, and only Rowan can block them. Keep him alive to win.
- **Trial 2 · Bout 4 — "The Longbow's Test":** Rowan tests the player with a Kingdom ranger deck full of Reach and small Scorch effects. He picks off weak champions every turn, so the player must play sturdy ones.
- **Trial 3 · Bout 2 — "The Wind Argues" (Edict in play):** The Edict gives each champion with Reach −1 Might, but +1 Might for every turn it has stayed on the field.

# Kael — Seeker of the Crown
*Kingdom · Duelist · Tier I · Chapter I · The First Light*

> Status: PROPOSED lore (draft for Jerian's approval). Canon quotes are marked [canon].

## Who they are

"Everyone in the Crownfall wants the Crown. Kael is the only one who wants to be worthy of it." [canon]

Kael is a young swordsman in travelling leathers, with a borrowed sword, a patched cloak and no title to his name. He did not come to the Crownfall to win. He came to become the kind of person who would deserve to.

He is not the strongest champion. He may be the most honest.

## Before the Crownfall

Kael grew up in the Kingdom realm, in a village too small to appear on royal maps. His mother kept the village forge. His father was a soldier who went to war for a king and did not come back.

He trained with an old wooden sword, then a real one bought with three summers of farm work.

## Why they answered the call

Kael left home with a borrowed sword. He answered the call of a Sovereign because he believed the Crown should go to someone who deserves it, and that the only way to know what deserving looks like is to stand in the Arena and find out.

He does not say whether he hopes it will be him. He says: "I will be worthy." [canon] It is not quite the same thing.

## The Ascension

It was a hard bout against a famous duelist, and Kael was losing fairly. He had a chance to win unfairly: his rival had slipped, back turned, and the crowd was shouting for him to strike.

He stepped back and waited for his rival to stand.

The crowd went silent. Above the Arena, the Crown's shards slowed in their orbit, as if listening.

His leathers hardened into blue-steel armour, bright and plain, with no crest at all. His sword drew a crescent of light through the air each time it moved, a pale arc that hung for a heartbeat before fading.

"Stronger — for the Crown." [canon]

He won the bout cleanly, his crescents sweeping across every rival champion at once. Afterwards, the duelist he had spared came to find him. "You could have won faster," she said. Kael nodded. "I could have won worse," he said. That night he wrote a new line in his book: *The Crown noticed.*

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension

Kael's Awakening came on a day he did not win. His Sovereign's banner lost the bout, and Kael stayed on the sand afterwards to help carry the wounded of both sides off the field. The Arena was nearly empty. The light was going.

As he set down the last injured rival, he felt a weight settle on his brow. There was no crown there. It only felt like one.

His armour took on the look of a hero-in-waiting, worn and royal at once. His sword blazed with a sigil he had never seen: a symbol of a kingdom that does not exist yet. He did not know whose kingdom it was. He suspects, sometimes, that it might be one he will build.

He looked up at the Crown and said nothing for a long time. Then he picked up his sword and walked home to his banner.

"One step closer." [canon]

## Voice and temperament

Kael is earnest, polite and quietly stubborn. He speaks plainly and avoids boasting. Even his battle cry is a promise to himself: "I will be worthy." [canon] When he wins, he never celebrates; he only says, "One step closer." [canon]

## Bonds and rivalries

- **Aurelia** — Aurelia already wears a crown, and Kael has spent most of his life distrusting people who do. She finds him exhausting and slightly inspiring. He finds her fairer than he expected.
- **Rowan** — Another Kingdom champion of humble birth. Rowan is the friend who keeps Kael from taking himself too seriously, and Kael is the friend who makes Rowan want to aim higher.
- **Crownless** — Kael has heard the story that someone once won the Crownfall and refused the Crown. He has asked everyone about it. When he finally met Crownless, he could not think of a single question to ask.

## Beneath the Arena (Set 2)

When the banners went down beneath the Arena, Kael went looking for something he could not name. In the torchlit vaults, among broken plinths and forgotten weapons, he found old relics of champions who had come before, some worthy, some not.

Below, he became the **Relic Seeker**, a Delver who studies what the past left behind. His ability, **Delve**, sorts the useful from the useless: he draws, keeps what matters, and sends the rest to the bottom of the pile. As he rises, he brings lost Relics back from the Fallen pile.

## In the campaign

- **Trial 1 · Bout 4 — "A Fair Fight":** A puzzle Bout. The rival has one large champion. The player must defeat it without attacking the rival's Crown Shards until the champion falls. Breaking a shard early fails the Bout. Teaches patience and Scorch.
- **Trial 2 · Bout 1 — "The Seeker's Test":** Kael tests the player with a Kingdom deck of steady duelists. The lesson is good blocking and trading.
- **Trial 3 · Bout 3 — "Worthy" (Edict in play):** The Edict gives champions +1 Might when they attack a champion and −1 Might when they attack a Crown Shard. Kael fights on the player's side.

# Solenne Ash — Knight of the Burning Dawn
*Dawnforged · Vanguard · Tier II · Chapter I · The First Light*

> Status: PROPOSED lore (draft for Jerian's approval). Canon quotes are marked [canon].

## Who they are

"Solenne swore to burn away every shadow in the Crownfall. She has started a list." [canon]

Solenne Ash is a flame knight in rose-gold plate, and she is exactly as direct as she looks. She keeps her list in a small, battered notebook.

She charges first and asks questions while she is charging.

## Before the Crownfall

Solenne was born in the Dawnforged realm, in a border fort on the western cliffs where the sun sets and the night comes in from the sea. Her family were Dawn Wardens, who light the beacon fires there every dusk.

She was the youngest Warden ever to stand a night watch alone. On that watch, something came out of the dark that the beacon could not frighten away. Solenne held the cliff until morning. She never said what it was. The next day, she bought the notebook.

## Why they answered the call

The Crownfall draws champions from every realm and age, and with them come shadows from every realm and age. To Solenne, the Arena looks like one enormous watchtower with every darkness in the world gathered under it. She answered a Sovereign's call because it was the quickest way inside.

## The Ascension

It was a night bout, which the Arena allows only rarely. Torches ringed the sand. Solenne's Sovereign had put her in front, as always, and the rival had sent something made of shadow and teeth straight at her line.

She raised her blade. The torches guttered. For a moment, every light in the Arena went out, and Solenne stood in perfect dark, exactly as she had on the western cliff years ago.

She was not afraid this time. She was annoyed.

Her plume caught fire first, a crest of real flame streaming back from her helm. Then her blade went white, so hot the sand beneath it turned to glass. The darkness flinched.

"The dawn burns brighter!" [canon]

She charged before the torches relit, and they relit because of her. When it was over, she took out her notebook in the glow of her own sword and crossed off one line. Her hands were shaking. She told herself it was from the heat.

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension

The second time the Crown raised her, there was no darkness at all. It was noon, the hardest light of the day, and her own line was breaking. Friends were falling. There was no shadow to burn, only a rival who was simply stronger.

She understood then that she had been fighting the wrong thing. The dawn is not only what drives the night away. It is what makes people stand up in the morning and try again.

Her plate rang like a forge. It became sun-forged armour, gold and rose and white. Blazing wings opened behind her, and when she moved, a trail of dawnfire followed her across the sand, warming everyone it passed. Her friends rose. Her charge carried all of them.

When the rival's last champion fell, she lowered her blade and said one word.

"Cleansed." [canon]

That night she opened her notebook and, for the first time, added something to a new page titled *Things worth protecting*.

## Voice and temperament

Solenne is blunt, loyal and impatient. She speaks in short, hard sentences and hates waiting. She has a dry sense of humour that most people miss. Her battle greeting is "Shadows, beware." [canon]

## Bonds and rivalries

- **Solara** — The young Dawnforged novice follows Solenne around, asking to see her list. Solenne pretends this is irritating. She has quietly added "anything that hurts Solara" to the top of page one.
- **Nyx** — A name on the list. Solenne has fought Nyx three times and lost the trail each time.
- **General Varr** — Varr taught Solenne that a charge is only as good as the line behind it. She respects him more than almost anyone, and argues with him constantly.

## Beneath the Arena (Set 2)

When the banners went down beneath the Arena, Solenne was the first to volunteer. The deep places were the darkest she had ever seen, which meant they needed her most.

Below, she became the **Lantern of the Underways**, a Delver whose blade lights the tunnels so that others can follow. Her ability, **Lantern Path**, shows the way forward: every time she arrives or rises, her banner learns a little more about the dark ahead, and at her brightest she shields everyone walking behind her.

Her list has grown very long. Some of the new entries have no names, only drawings.

## In the campaign

- **Trial 1 · Bout 2 — "Night Watch":** A puzzle Bout. The rival has only shadow-type champions with Ambush. The player is given Solenne and must use Charge to strike before the ambushers do. Teaches Charge.
- **Trial 2 · Bout 3 — "The List":** Solenne tests the player with an aggressive Dawnforged deck full of Charge and Scorch.
- **Trial 3 · Bout 1 — "Burn the Dark" (Edict in play):** An Edict prevents Shields this Bout. Solenne fights beside the player as an ally card handed out at the start, and the player must win before the rival's numbers wear her down.

# Elara — Field Surgeon of the Realm
*Kingdom · Mystic · Tier II · Chapter I · The First Light*

> Status: PROPOSED lore (draft for Jerian's approval). Canon quotes are marked [canon].

## Who they are

"Elara has saved more soldiers than any general has led." [canon]

Elara is a battlefield healer with a satchel of salves, rolled-up sleeves and a voice that cuts through noise. She is not a priestess and does not pretend to be one. Her magic is real, but she trusts clean bandages and steady hands just as much.

She does not care much about the Crown. She cares about who is still standing when the bout is done.

## Before the Crownfall

Elara grew up in the Kingdom realm, the daughter of a village herbalist. When the Kingdom went to war on its northern border, she followed the army as a helper in the field tents, boiling water and rolling bandages. Within a year she was running the tents.

She learned her light magic late, from an old book left behind by a travelling healer.

The war ended. Elara did not go home. She went wherever soldiers still got hurt.

## Why they answered the call

The Crownfall is full of champions who fight, fall and fight again. To Elara, it looked like the biggest field hospital in history, with no one properly in charge. She answered a Sovereign's call because she wanted a place on the field, not in the stands.

She also has a quieter reason. She has heard that the Crown can raise champions into more than they were. She wants to know if it can be taught to heal.

## The Ascension

The bout had gone badly. Three champions of her banner lay on the sand, and the rival was pressing hard. Elara was on her knees between them with one hand on a wound and the other holding her tome open on the ground.

"No," she said, to nobody in particular. "Not today."

The book began to glow brighter than it ever had, so bright it lit her face from below. Her dusty field coat became white-and-blue royal vestments, clean as a fresh sheet. The light from the tome spilled across the sand and found each fallen champion, one by one, and lifted them.

"No one falls today." [canon]

She stood. Her banner stood with her.

Afterwards, Elara sat alone for a long time, the tome closed on her knees. She had always believed that healing came from skill and care. Now the Crown had answered her. She was grateful. She was also, just a little, afraid of how much she wanted it to answer again.

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension

Her Awakening came in a bout that should have been a disaster. The rival's champions had swept the field, and Elara was the last of her banner left standing. Everyone she had mended was gone from the field.

She did not run. She opened her tome to the last page, the one that had always been blank.

Light poured out of it. It rose like a column, then spread like wings, and Elara stood in the middle of it, looking for a moment like an angel of the field hospital. The light found the fallen of her banner and called them back to her, two of them at once. Then it wrapped every champion she had in a bright shield.

The rival's attack broke against that shield like rain on glass.

When the bout was over, Elara walked the field and counted heads, the way she always does.

"Everyone accounted for." [canon]

## Voice and temperament

Elara is brisk, practical and warm underneath. She gives orders to generals without noticing. She has no patience for heroics that get people hurt. Her first words on any field are the same: "Who needs mending?" [canon]

## Bonds and rivalries

- **General Varr** — Elara has patched up Varr more times than either can count. He calls her the finest soldier in the Kingdom. She tells him to sit still.
- **Solara** — Two healers from different worlds. Solara prays while she heals; Elara stitches while she talks. They have learned a lot from each other, though Elara would never admit to praying.
- **Grizz** — Grizz once wandered into her field tent with a thorn in his paw and refused to leave until he was given a biscuit. He now visits after every bout. Elara pretends to be annoyed.

## Beneath the Arena (Set 2)

When the banners went down beneath the Arena, the climb back up became the hardest part. Explorers fell in the tunnels, and some halls collapsed with champions still inside.

Elara went down after them. Below, she became the **Healer of the Fallen Deep**, a Restorer tending the wounded by lantern light in a collapsed hall. Her ability, **Field Mending**, shields friends when she arrives and mends what the deep breaks. At her strongest, she still does what she always did: she brings the fallen back into the fight.

She has set up a field tent under the Arena.

## In the campaign

- **Trial 1 · Bout 6 — "Triage":** A puzzle Bout. The player starts with three champions in the Fallen pile and Elara in hand. Return the right champion at the right time to win in four turns. Teaches the Fallen pile.
- **Trial 2 · Bout 5 — "The Surgeon's Test":** Elara tests the player with a Kingdom deck that keeps bringing champions back.
- **Trial 3 · Bout 4 — "No One Falls" (Edict in play):** The Edict says the first champion each player loses each turn returns to their hand. Elara is an ally card. Win with every champion still alive for a bonus reward.

# Aurelia — Crown Commander
*Kingdom · Commander · Tier III · Chapter I · The First Light*

> Status: PROPOSED lore (draft for Jerian's approval). Canon quotes are marked [canon].

## Who they are

"Aurelia already has a crown. She wants to know why this one is bigger." [canon]

Aurelia is a royal battle commander in blue and gold, and she is used to being obeyed. She is brave, sharp and proud, and she is not impressed by a Crown just because it floats.

She is also curious. Underneath the pride is a ruler who genuinely wants to understand what makes one crown matter more than another. She suspects the answer will tell her something about her own.

## Before the Crownfall

Aurelia is a princess of the Kingdom realm, the eldest child of its ruling house. She was trained in war, law and diplomacy from the age of six. The Kingdom's people love her because she rides at the front of her own banners.

She grew up knowing that her crown was inherited, not earned. It bothered her more than she let anyone see.

Then the Kingdom's scholars brought word of the Crown above the Arena: a crown that cannot be inherited, only won.

## Why they answered the call

Aurelia's council argued for weeks about whether the Kingdom should send a champion. Aurelia ended the argument by going herself. Her reason is simple. She wants to know why this crown is bigger. She wants to know if it judges the wearer. And she wants to know, though she has never said so aloud, whether hers would pass that test.

## The Ascension

It was the first great battle the Kingdom's banner fought in the Arena. Aurelia had placed her champions well, but the rival was stronger, and her line was beginning to waver. She saw the doubt on their faces: young Kael, steady Rowan, old Varr.

She did what she has always done. She rode to the front.

"Rally to me!" [canon]

The Crown's shards flashed. Behind her, as if from nowhere, a standard bearer appeared, marching in step with the Kingdom's colours raised high. Her circlet began to glow with a soft gold light, and that light seemed to spread to every champion who looked at it.

Her line stopped wavering. Every champion behind her fought harder and stood taller.

"For the realm!" [canon]

They won. That evening, Aurelia took off her circlet and studied it by candlelight for a long time. It had never glowed before. She did not know whether the Crown had honoured it or simply noticed it. She found she cared a great deal about the difference.

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension

Her Awakening came when the Kingdom's banner faced champions from three realms at once. Aurelia was outnumbered, her champions tired, the Arena loud with rival cheers.

She raised her banner and did not give a speech. She thought of her father's throne, of every law she had signed, of every soldier who had ever followed her. Then she simply stood still and let them see her.

The Crown answered. Light poured into her circlet until she looked crowned in it, a crown made of light. Her banner rose upward and became a column of gold, so tall it could be seen from every platform in the Arena. Every champion on her side felt it: they hit harder, and they stood firmer.

The rival champions broke against that column of gold and did not get up.

"Victory for the Crown." [canon]

She said it as she always does. Only afterwards did she wonder which crown she meant.

## Voice and temperament

Aurelia is commanding, quick-witted and proud, with a strong sense of fairness. She expects excellence and gives it. She can be cold with those she does not respect, and fiercely loyal to those she does. Her orders are short and carry far: "Rally to me!" [canon]

## Bonds and rivalries

- **Kael** — The young swordsman who distrusts anyone wearing a crown. Aurelia finds him exhausting. She also finds herself checking, before every decision, whether Kael would call it worthy. She would rather lose a bout than tell him so.
- **General Varr** — Varr served Aurelia's father and taught Aurelia to hold a shield. He has never been given the Crown, or any crown.
- **Sol** — Aurelia tried to question Sol about the Crown's true nature. Sol smiled and told her that crowns are very new things. Aurelia did not enjoy being called new.

## Beneath the Arena (Set 2)

When something stirred beneath the Arena, Aurelia was the first commander to decide the Kingdom would go and see. She led the Kingdom's banners down the great stair beneath the Arena, torches lit, colours raised.

Below, she became the **Leader of the Descent**, a Delver who leads from the front into the unknown. Her ability, **Banner of the Descent**, keeps her champions informed and ready: she draws as she arrives, lifts every Kingdom champion's strength, and at her strongest readies another champion to strike again.

## In the campaign

- **Trial 2 · Bout 6 — "The Commander's Test":** Aurelia tests the player with a Kingdom deck built around boosting Might. The lesson: remove the commander first.
- **Trial 3 · Bout 5 — "Rally to Me" (Edict in play):** The Edict gives each player's champions +1 Might for every other champion they control. Aurelia, Kael, Rowan and Varr fight as a team on the player's side.
- **Trial 1 · Bout 7 — "Two Crowns":** Aurelia's introduction. She challenges the player to prove their banner is worth the Kingdom's time. Win without losing more than two Crown Shards.

# General Varr — The Unbroken Line
*Kingdom · Commander, Vanguard · Tier IV · Chapter I · The First Light*

> Status: PROPOSED lore (draft for Jerian's approval). Canon quotes are marked [canon].

## Who they are

"Varr has held every line he was given. He was never given the Crown." [canon]

General Varr is a grizzled kingdom commander with a tower shield, a grey beard and a voice like a falling wall. He has fought in more wars than most champions have lived years. He is not flashy and does not try to be. He stands where he is told to stand, and nothing gets past him.



## Before the Crownfall

Varr was born in the Kingdom realm to a family of farmers. He joined the royal army at fifteen because the harvest had failed and the army paid in bread. He never left.

He held the Bridge of Tolls for nine days against an army five times his size, and a hundred lines after it. Each time, he was given a medal, a handshake and a new line to hold.

He was never made a lord. He was never offered a crown. He says he never wanted one. People who know him well are not so sure.

## Why they answered the call

The Kingdom sent its best to the Crownfall, and Varr was among them, as always. Officially, he answered a Sovereign's call to protect Princess Aurelia and the Kingdom's younger champions.

He has spent his whole life holding lines for crowns other people wore. Somewhere in the back of his mind, he wants to stand in front of the one crown that cannot be inherited and simply ask it: was that worth something?

## The Ascension

It was a brutal bout. The rival had three champions with Charge, all aimed at Varr's line. His Sovereign's banner was down to its last Crown Shard. Behind Varr stood young Kael and a half-trained archer, Rowan.

Varr set his tower shield in the sand and planted his feet.

"Form up!" [canon]

The first charge hit him and stopped. The second hit him and stopped. On the third, something changed. The weight of every war he had fought seemed to settle on his shoulders, and it did not crush him. It steadied him.

His plain uniform shone with medals, one for every war he had held, each glinting gold in the sun. A banner-spear rose in his hand, the Kingdom's colours snapping above him. Every champion behind him felt a shield fall over them like a warm blanket.

"The line does not break." [canon]

It did not. Afterwards, Kael asked him how he did it. Varr shrugged and said, "Same as always. I stayed."

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension

Years of battle came together in a single bout. The Kingdom's banner faced a Sovereign whose champions seemed endless. Varr was wounded. Elara had begged him to sit it out. He refused.

As the rival's final assault came, Varr raised his shield and felt the Arena go very quiet.

Beside him, figures began to form out of the dust: shieldbearers, translucent and glowing faintly, in the uniforms of every war he had fought. Some he recognised. Some he had buried. They raised their shields beside his without a word.

He did not ask how. He did not ask why. He simply gave the order he had given a thousand times.

"Hold the line!" [canon]

Every champion behind him stood behind a wall of phantom shields, and the rival's assault broke on it like a wave on a cliff. When the dust settled, the phantoms were gone. Varr lowered his shield.

"Line held." [canon]

## Voice and temperament

Varr is gruff, patient and steady. He speaks in short, plain sentences and never boasts. He is gentle with the young and stern with the careless. He remembers every soldier he lost. His orders never change: "Form up!" [canon] And when it is over, only two words: "Line held." [canon]

## Bonds and rivalries

- **Aurelia** — Varr served her family for three generations and taught her to hold a shield. He would give his life for her. He would also tell her, to her face, when she is wrong.
- **Elara** — The field surgeon who has patched him up more times than either can count. He calls her the finest soldier in the Kingdom.
- **Rowan** — Varr wants archers in rows. Rowan wanders off to find better angles. Varr grumbles every time and has never once been wrong to trust him.

## Beneath the Arena (Set 2)

When the banners went down beneath the Arena, somebody had to stay at the gate. Varr did not need to be asked.

Below, he became the **Holder of the Gate**, a Sentinel holding the last gate between the Arena and what lies beneath. His ability, **Last Gate**, weakens anything that tries to reach his banner's Crown: rival champions attacking it are weaker, Kingdom allies around him are tougher, and at his strongest he shields every champion he has.

He has not seen what is down there. He does not need to. His job is that it does not come up.

# Sol — The First Light
*Dawnforged · Ascendant, Mystic · Tier V · Chapter I · The First Light (The Crowning)*

> Status: PROPOSED lore (draft for Jerian's approval). Canon quotes are marked [canon].

## Who they are

"Before anything, there was Sol. Sol would like it back that way." [canon]

Sol looks like the first light given a body: a tall, bright figure whose edges are hard to see, as if someone were standing in front of the morning sun. Sol walks slowly, speaks softly and seems faintly amused by almost everything.

No one agrees on what Sol is. The Dawnforged temples sing that Sol is the first sunrise. Kingdom scholars say Sol is simply a very old mystic who has had a long time to practise. Some champions whisper that Sol is not a champion at all.

## Before the Crownfall

This is where the stories stop agreeing.

The Dawnforged say that before the realms had names, there was only one morning, and it was Sol. They say the light was taken, or broken, or given away, and that Sol has been waiting ever since. Solara's order goes further and says the Crown itself is that first sunrise, stolen.

The Kingdom's oldest books mention a "bright traveller" who appears at the start of great ages and leaves before they end.

Sol, asked directly, said only: "I remember the dark. It was very quiet."

## Why they answered the call

Sol's canon lore is the only clue anyone has: Sol would like it back the way it was before anything. What "it" means, Sol will not say.

Why a being that old would need a Sovereign at all is a question many have asked. Sol only smiles.

## The Ascension

No living champion saw Sol's first Ascension. But many saw the day Sol was raised again in the Arena, in front of every Sovereign, and they describe it the same way.

It was dawn. The Arena was cold and grey. Sol walked to the centre of the sand, alone, and looked up at the Crown. The shards stopped turning. For one long breath, nothing in the Arena moved, not even the wind.

Then light gathered behind Sol in a ring, a corona as bright as noon. Four wings of light unfolded from it, slow and enormous, until their tips brushed the Arena walls. Every champion on Sol's banner was wrapped in a shield of warm gold. The cold went out of the morning.

"Brighter than the first morning." [canon]

Solara, watching from the stands, began to cry and did not know why. Sol looked up at the Crown for a moment longer, as if waiting for an answer. If one came, Sol never shared it.

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension

Sol's Awakening is the moment every champion in the Crownfall hopes not to face from the other side.

It happened during a Crowning, late in the day, with the sun already low. Sol's Sovereign was nearly beaten. The rival's champions had surrounded Sol, and the light around the old being had dimmed to a candle's glow.

Sol closed their eyes and smiled.

"Let there be DAWN!" [canon]

A miniature sun rose above the Arena. It was small enough to fit between the towers and bright enough to turn night into noon. Its heat swept across the sand and scorched every rival champion at once. Then the same light turned gentle and mended a broken shard of Sol's own claim.

When it faded, Sol was standing alone in the centre of the sand.

"The light endures." [canon]

Some of the Sovereigns watching that day say Sol looked sad. Others say Sol looked like someone remembering.

## Voice and temperament

Sol is calm, gentle, ancient and quietly unsettling. Sol rarely raises their voice and never seems hurried. Sol's greeting to a Sovereign is "Before anything, there was me." [canon] It sounds more like a reminder.

## Bonds and rivalries

- **Solara** — The young novice whose order sings hymns about the first light. Sol is kind to her and has never answered any of her questions.
- **Aurex** — The last dragon and the other great legend of the first two chapters. Aurex and Sol are very old and very wary of each other. Aurex once said Sol "remembers too much and says too little." Sol said Aurex "says too much and remembers it all."
- **Noctara** — Light and night. Sol and Noctara have never fought in the Arena. When they pass each other, both stop, and neither speaks.

## Beneath the Arena (Set 2)

When the banners went down beneath the Arena, Sol went deeper than anyone. Champions who followed found Sol in the deepest cavern of all, standing in total darkness, then saw the first light rising inside it, turning the dark to gold.

Below, Sol became the **Dawn Beneath the World**, an Ascendant and Restorer whose ability, **SECOND DAWN**, mends, shields and lifts an entire banner at once. Why Sol's light rises so easily in a place that has never seen the sun, Sol does not say.

# Fen — Blood of the Pack
*Wildkin · Duelist · Tier I · Chapter II · Blood and Ember*

> Status: PROPOSED lore (draft for Jerian's approval). Canon quotes are marked [canon].

## Who they are

"Fen tracks weakness. He learned it from the winters, and the winters were thorough." [canon]

Fen is a lean grey wolf warrior with bone-hilted claws and eyes that never stop moving.

He is not cruel. To Fen, hunting is not about hate. It is about surviving, and about making sure the pack gets home.

## Before the Crownfall

Fen was born in the Wildkin realm, in the deep northern forests where winter lasts most of the year. His pack lived by the old rules: hunt together, eat together, leave no one behind. Fen was the runt of his litter, small and thin, and the winters nearly took him more than once.

He survived by learning. He learned which deer were tired, which ice was thin, which storms would pass and which would kill.

Then came the longest winter anyone remembered. Fen led the pack through it. Not all of them made it. He still counts the ones who did not.

## Why they answered the call

The Crownfall is a long way from the northern forests. Fen came because the Wildkin elders said the Crown could make a realm strong.

He answered a Sovereign's call because a lone wolf cannot claim anything. Fen understands packs. A Sovereign, to him, is just a new alpha, one who needs to earn his trust.

## The Ascension

It was a night bout under a red moon. The Arena sometimes turns the sky strange, and that night the moon hung low and crimson above the Crown. Fen's banner was losing badly. His Sovereign had only two Crown Shards left, and the rival had five.

Fen did not panic. He watched. He saw the rival champion who was tired, the one guarding the left too much, the one who had stopped paying attention because they thought they had already won.

He howled once, long and low.

Crimson war paint began to glow across his muzzle as if the red moon had painted it there. Scars along his arms hardened, and hide armour wrapped around his shoulders. He felt every one of the old winters standing behind him.

"The pack grows." [canon]

He struck from the shadows, where no one was looking. The tired champion fell first. Then the careless one. His banner was still behind, but Fen was strongest when it was. That was how he had always lived.

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension

The second red moon came late in the season, during a bout against champions far bigger than Fen. He was hurt and slowing. His Sovereign was losing, and the rival's champions were closing in on every side. He thought of the long winter and the wolves he could not bring home.

Something ran beside him.

It was a wolf made of red light, larger than any wolf alive, silent and swift. Every time Fen leapt, the spirit-wolf leapt with him. Every time Fen struck, the spirit struck too. He did not know if it was one of his lost pack, or the moon, or the Crown. He did not stop to ask.

He was an alpha of the blood moon now. He brought down one champion and was ready again before it hit the sand, then another, then another.

When the bout ended, the spirit-wolf turned once, looked at him and faded.

"Good hunt." [canon]

## Voice and temperament

Fen is quiet, watchful and blunt. He speaks in short phrases and rarely smiles. He is fiercely loyal to his pack, including any banner that has earned his trust. His greeting tells you exactly what he is thinking: "I smell weakness." [canon]

## Bonds and rivalries

- **Grizz** — The enormous bear and Fen are both Wildkin, and they are an odd pair. Fen hunts; Grizz naps. Fen thinks Grizz is lazy. Grizz thinks Fen is grumpy. In battle, they fight back to back without needing a word.
- **Rask** — The pit champion of Sszar counts in threes. Fen counts tired breaths. They respect each other's skill and do not trust each other at all. Every time they meet, each tries to find the other's weakness.
- **Pyrax** — Fen once tracked Pyrax for two days through the Arena's outer gardens, thinking she was a monster. When he found her, she offered him half her lunch. He has never quite recovered from the embarrassment.

## Beneath the Arena (Set 2)

When something stirred beneath the Arena, Fen smelled it first. It was a new scent, old and huge and hungry. He went down the tunnels before anyone told him to.

Below, he became the **Pack Hunter**, a Hunter running the dark tunnels with the scent of a monster ahead. His ability, **Scent of Prey**, makes him stronger against bigger things: Monsters, Guardians, and champions who have already Ascended. At his strongest, every kill tells him something new.

Fen says it is like the winters again. He says it like that is a good thing.

# Emberling — Spark That Learned to Speak
*Elemental · Arcanist · Tier I · Chapter II · Blood and Ember*

> Status: PROPOSED lore (draft for Jerian's approval). Canon quotes are marked [canon].

## Who they are

"She was a candle once. Then someone read a spell too close to her." [canon]

Emberling is a small living flame with curious coal eyes and a voice like crackling kindling. She is about the size of a teacup, floats wherever she likes and wants to know what everything feels like when it is on fire. She is cheerful, impatient and astonishingly brave for someone who can be put out with a cup of water.

## Before the Crownfall

Emberling was a candle on a scholar's desk in a tower of the Elemental realm, a land where fire, water, stone and storm have voices of their own. For years she simply burned, quietly, lighting pages of spell books and going out at bedtime.

One night the scholar read aloud a spell of awakening, much too close to the candle. Something caught. The flame flickered, blinked and said its first word. The word was "Oh."

The scholar fainted. Emberling explored. She learned to speak from the scholar's books, to float from watching moths, and to throw small fireballs from watching the hearth.

## Why they answered the call

Emberling heard about the Crownfall from a travelling fire elemental and immediately wanted to go. Everyone said she was too small. She took that as a challenge.

She answered a Sovereign's call because she wants to see everything, and the Crownfall has everything. If asked about the Crown, she says she wants to know if it is warm.

## The Ascension

It was her first real bout. Emberling had been told to stay at the back. Instead she floated straight to the front, where a big rival champion was about to crush one of her friends.

"Hey!" she said. Nobody heard her. She was very small.

So she got bigger.

She did not know she could do it. She just burned harder than she ever had, until the air around her crackled. Her flame turned blue at the tips, then all over the top, and a crown of blue-hot embers formed on her head. She was a crackling fire sprite now, bright and furious.

"I'm a real fire now!" [canon]

The rival champion stepped back, surprised. Emberling threw her first proper fireball, and it hit, and it hurt. Her friends cheered. Emberling did three loops in the air.

That night she asked the Crown if it could make her even bigger. The Crown did not answer. She decided that meant "maybe."

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension

Emberling's Awakening came during a bout against water champions, which she had always been secretly afraid of. One splash and she would be gone. She knew it. Everyone knew it. Her friends had told her to stay home.

The rival sent a great wave across the Arena towards her banner. Her friends could not get out of the way in time.

Emberling did not run. She took a deep breath, which flames are not supposed to be able to do, and burned.

She became a miniature sun-spirit. The air around her warped and shimmered with heat. When the wave reached her, it turned to steam and drifted away. Her fireballs flew out in spiralling trails, curling across the Arena, scorching every rival champion at once.

When the steam cleared, she was still there, glowing, very pleased with herself.

"Warm, warm, warm!" [canon]

She is still afraid of water. She is just less afraid than she was.

## Voice and temperament

Emberling is bubbly, curious, impulsive and kind-hearted. She talks fast and asks a lot of questions, most of them about fire. Her greeting is always "Can I burn something?" [canon] The answer is usually no. She asks anyway.

## Bonds and rivalries

- **Ignara** — The great fire elemental warrior. Emberling thinks Ignara is the most amazing person who ever lived and follows her everywhere. Ignara finds this embarrassing and has quietly taken Emberling under her protection.
- **Grizz** — Emberling once tried to set Grizz's fur on fire to see what would happen. He laughed so hard he fell over. Now they are best friends, and she sometimes rides on his head.
- **Mirella** — The water mage of the tides is very kind to Emberling, which Emberling finds terrifying. They are slowly becoming friends. Emberling keeps a respectful distance of three paces.

## Beneath the Arena (Set 2)

When the banners went down beneath the Arena, Emberling found the forge-vents: hot shafts of air rising from the deep forges, full of sparks. She flew into one and did not come out for a day.

When she did, she had changed. She became the **Ember Conduit**, a Channeler, a living spark drifting through the forge-vents with sparks spiralling up into her. Her ability, **Ember Echo**, means that every time her banner casts a Tactic, a little bit of fire leaps out and Scorches a rival. At her strongest, it scorches all of them.

She says the forges sing. She has been trying to learn the song.

# Grizz — The Mountain That Walks
*Wildkin · Bruiser · Tier II · Chapter II · Blood and Ember*

## Who they are

"Grizz does not understand the Crown. He understands that everyone wants it, and he likes a crowd." [canon]

Grizz is an enormous bear warrior with iron-shod claws, a belly laugh that rattles the stands and a vocabulary of roughly one word. He is the biggest champion most rivals have ever stood next to, and he is also, by every account, the friendliest.

He is not clever and does not pretend to be. But he is honest, and he is loyal, and nothing on earth will move him once he decides to stand still.

## Before the Crownfall

Grizz comes from the Wildkin realm, from the high mountain valleys where the bears live in caves that look out over the clouds.

As a cub he wandered down to the valley villages to watch the festivals. As a grown bear he wandered further, to the markets, the fairs, the wrestling pits. He wrestled anyone who asked and many who did not. He always shared his winnings, mostly in the form of honey cakes.

The other bears called him "the Mountain That Walks" because he never stayed home. He took it as a compliment.

## Why they answered the call

Nobody sent Grizz. He heard there was a giant floating place where champions from every realm met and fought in front of huge crowds. That was enough. He walked there.

He answered a Sovereign's call because the Sovereign had snacks. That is the honest version. The longer version is that Grizz has come to like the champions around him. He does not care about the Crown. He cares about the friends who want it.

## The Ascension

It was a crowded bout, the stands packed and the noise deafening. The rival had sent three champions straight at Grizz's banner, and Grizz was the only thing between them and his friends.

He did not attack. He just stood there, enormous, and let them come.

The first one bounced off him. The second one bounced off him. The third one he caught, lifted and set down very firmly. The crowd went wild.

Grizz loved it. The louder they cheered, the bigger he felt, and then he really was bigger. Bright war-paint spread across his face and shoulders. A heavy pauldron settled on his shoulder, made from the skull of a mountain troll he had once wrestled for three days.

He threw back his head and roared.

"BIGGER GRAHH!" [canon]

The crowd roared back. Grizz did not know what the Crown had done to him, exactly. He knew it felt like the best festival ever.

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension

Grizz's Awakening came on the day his friends were in real trouble. His banner was losing. Fen was down. The young champions were frightened. The crowd had gone quiet, and Grizz did not like quiet. Quiet meant his friends were scared.

He planted his feet. He took a breath so deep the banners around the Arena fluttered towards him.

Then he roared.

He grew as he roared, taller and wider, until he was a primal bear-titan, an old mountain spirit given fur and claws. His roar shook the Arena floor. Stones cracked. Rival champions stumbled. Every attacker who came at him was knocked flat, and he struck back twice as hard.

When it was over, the crowd was roaring louder than ever. Grizz sat down in the middle of the sand, yawned enormously and closed his eyes.

"Nap time." [canon]

His friends had to step around him for the rest of the afternoon.

## Voice and temperament

Grizz is loud, warm, simple and joyful. He communicates mostly through roars, hugs and offering people food. His greeting is "GRAHH!" [canon] He is gentle with small creatures and children, protective of friends and mildly confused by anything involving plans.

## Bonds and rivalries

- **Fen** — The quiet wolf hunter. Grizz thinks Fen needs more naps. Fen thinks Grizz needs fewer. In battle they fight back to back and never need a plan.
- **Emberling** — The little living flame once tried to set Grizz's fur on fire to see what would happen. Grizz laughed so hard he fell over. They have been best friends ever since.
- **Elara** — The field surgeon took a thorn out of his paw once. He has visited her tent after every bout since, and he always brings a biscuit for her and asks for one back.

## Beneath the Arena (Set 2)

When the banners went down beneath the Arena, there were no crowds. Grizz found this very sad. Then he found the monsters, and he cheered up enormously.

Below, he became the **Breaker of Beasts**, a Hunter wrestling monsters twice his size in the caverns beneath the Arena. His ability, **Beastbreaker**, makes him stronger against Monsters, Guardians and champions who have already Ascended. When he wins, he gets tougher.

Grizz has started bringing defeated monsters back up to the surface, gently, so the crowd can see them. He is told this is not necessary. He keeps doing it.

# Mirella — Voice of the Tides
*Elemental · Mystic · Tier II · Chapter II · Blood and Ember*

> Status: PROPOSED lore (draft for Jerian's approval). Canon quotes are marked [canon].

## Who they are

"Mirella can hear every ocean. The Crownfall has none, and the silence frightens her." [canon]

Mirella is a water mage who carries a swirling orb of seawater in one hand, the way other people carry a lantern. She is always listening to the sea. Every ocean in every realm sings to her, faintly, all the time.

Except here. The floating kingdom of the Crownfall hangs high above everything, and no ocean's voice reaches it. For the first time in her life, Mirella hears silence.

## Before the Crownfall

Mirella comes from the Elemental realm, from the island chains of the Tidelands, where the water itself is alive and speaks to those who will listen. Her people are tide-singers. They calm storms, guide ships home and settle disputes between the sea and the shore.

Mirella was born on a boat during a storm, and the storm stopped the moment she cried. She grew up learning the songs of currents and the moods of waves. By twenty she could hear oceans in other realms, far away and faint, like music in another room.

## Why they answered the call

One day, every ocean she could hear fell briefly silent at once. It lasted only a heartbeat. In that heartbeat, she felt a pull towards the sky, towards the floating kingdom where the Crown hangs.

She does not know what it was. She does not know if the Crown caused it. She answered a Sovereign's call because she needed to find out, and because she is afraid that if it happens again, the silence might not end.

## The Ascension

It was a hard bout, in a dry season, when the Arena's sand was so hot it shimmered. Mirella's banner was being scorched by fire champions, and her orb of seawater was nearly gone, boiled away to a few drops.

She closed her eyes and listened, the way she had on the islands. There was nothing. Only silence.

Then, very faintly, she heard something. It was not an ocean. It was the water in her friends: in their breath, their tears, their sweat. Tiny tides, everywhere.

She sang to them.

Foam rose around her and wove itself into robes of living, shifting water. A coral circlet grew across her brow, pink and white. Her orb refilled and spilled over, and a cool shield of water wrapped every champion on her banner.

"Every ocean sings with me." [canon]

She opened her eyes. The silence was still there. But now she knew she could sing into it.

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension

Mirella's Awakening came during a bout when the Arena's Edict forbade all healing. Her banner was taking blow after blow. She could not mend, could not soothe. She felt useless.

So she stopped trying to heal, and simply called the sea.

It came. Not a real ocean, but a ring of water rising up from nowhere to orbit around her, slow and shining, a full circle as tall as a wall. Through it swam fish made of light, darting and glittering, as if the ring were a window into some deep and distant sea.

She was a tide queen now. The water shielded every friend around her, and when the Edict ended, it brought a fresh card to her hand as if the tide had washed it in.

When the bout was over, the ring sank gently back into the sand, and the fish of light swam away.

"Calm waters." [canon]

## Voice and temperament

Mirella is gentle, thoughtful, a little shy and deeply caring. She speaks in calm, flowing sentences. Her greeting is "The tide turns." [canon] She is braver than she believes, and loyal to the end.

## Bonds and rivalries

- **Ignara** — Fire and water. Ignara considers everywhere else cold; Mirella finds everywhere Ignara goes far too dry. They argue constantly and fight brilliantly together.
- **Emberling** — The little fire spark keeps three paces away from Mirella at all times. Mirella finds this very sweet and has been trying, gently, to close the gap.
- **Captain Blacktide** — A pirate captain who sails seas that exist only between realities. Mirella can hear those strange seas faintly whenever he is near. They are the only waters she hears in the Crownfall, so she has started standing near Blacktide just to listen. He has not charged her a toll. Yet.

Below, she became the **Tide of the Sunken Halls**, a Restorer rising from the underground lake with healing water spilling up the stone stairs. Her ability, **Sunken Tide**, shields her friends as she arrives, and at her strongest she both shields and mends. The lake does not sing like the oceans do. It hums, low and slow.

She has not told anyone what she thinks it is humming about.

# Rask — Pit Champion of Sszar
*Wildkin · Duelist · Tier II · Chapter II · Blood and Ember*

> Status: PROPOSED lore (draft for Jerian's approval). Canon quotes are marked [canon].

## Who they are

"Rask counts in threes. So do his opponents, briefly." [canon]

Rask is a reptilian gladiator with a notched gladius, cool green scales and a slow, unblinking stare. He fights in a rhythm only he can hear: step, step, strike. One, two, three.

He is a showman and a professional. He loves the crowd, but he does not fight for it. He fights because it is the thing he is best at in all the world, and he has never been anything else.

## Before the Crownfall

Rask comes from the Wildkin realm, from the hot desert city of Sszar, where the reptile clans settle their arguments in great sand pits instead of wars. The pit fighters of Sszar are its heroes.

Rask was born to nobody in particular. He fought his first pit bout at fourteen for a bowl of rice and a blanket. He lost. He fought again the next day, and the day after that. Slowly, he learned the rhythm: patience, timing, three clean strokes. By twenty, he was undefeated. By twenty-five, they called him the Pit Champion of Sszar.

He grew bored. The best fighters of Sszar had all faced him. None of them had made him count past three.

## Why they answered the call

When word reached Sszar of an Arena floating above the realms, where champions from every age fought for a Crown, Rask packed his gladius and his trophy belt and left that night.

He answered a Sovereign's call because the Crownfall is the biggest pit there has ever been. He wants to know if anyone there can make him count to four.

## The Ascension

It was his tenth bout in the Arena. He was behind, as he often let himself be; Rask fights better when the crowd thinks he is losing. His Sovereign had two Crown Shards left. The rival's champions were grinning.

Rask began to count.

One. He stepped forward, and the first rival fell.

Two. He turned, and the second rival fell.

Three. He looked up, and the crowd had started chanting.

They were chanting his name. They did not even know why. Something in the Arena, or the Crown, had heard the rhythm and joined in.

His plain pit armour shone gold, polished like the shields of the old Sszar champions. A trophy belt settled around his waist, heavy with tokens from every bout he had ever won.

"The crowd chants my name." [canon]

He said it quietly, almost surprised. Then he went back to work, ready again after every win.

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension

Rask's Awakening came in the longest bout he had ever fought. His rival was a champion who did not fall on three, or on four, or on five. Rask was tired, his rhythm broken, his scales scratched. For the first time in years he wondered if he might lose.

He smiled. He had come all this way to wonder that.

His scales turned to gold, every one, gleaming like coins in the sun. Across his shoulders fell a long cloak, stitched from the colours of every champion he had ever bested, a patchwork of banners from every corner of the realms.

He breathed slowly, in and out, and listened. He found the rhythm again. Not one, two, three. Something deeper, slower, older.

When it was over, he bowed to his fallen rival, helped them up and walked off the sand to the roar of the crowd.

"Undefeated." [canon]

He has added the rival's colours to his cloak.

## Voice and temperament

Rask is calm, confident and a little theatrical. He speaks slowly, with a faint hiss, and counts aloud before fighting: "One. Two. Three." [canon] He respects skill above everything and is courteous to opponents who fight well. He is quietly lonely, though he would never say so.

## Bonds and rivalries

- **Fen** — The wolf hunter who tracks weakness. Rask and Fen keep trying to find each other's weak spots. Neither has.
- **Kael** — The young Kingdom swordsman once spared a rival in a bout Rask was watching. Rask did not understand it. He went to ask Kael why. They talked for hours. Rask still does not understand, but he respects it.
- **Grizz** — Grizz loves the crowd, and so does Rask. They once wrestled in the outer gardens for an audience of three Sovereigns and a gardener. Rask lost count. He considers it the best fight of his life.

## Beneath the Arena (Set 2)

When the banners went down beneath the Arena, Rask found something that made him laugh out loud: a pit. Deep under the Crownfall, in a cavern full of blood-red sand, there were old fighting pits, older than Sszar.

Below, he became the **Champion of the Underpits**, a Gladiator roaring in the red sand with a monster's skull at his feet. His ability, **Pit Fighter**, makes him stronger whenever he attacks a champion. When he wins, he is shielded, and at his best he is ready to fight again straight away.

He has started bringing a crowd down with him.

## In the campaign

- **Trial 1 · Bout 3 — "One, Two, Three":** A puzzle Bout. The player must defeat exactly three rival champions in a single turn using Rask's ready ability. Teaches readying.
- **Trial 2 · Bout 3 — "The Pit Champion's Test":** Rask tests the player with a Wildkin gladiator deck that gets stronger when behind.
- **Trial 3 · Bout 3 — "The Pits of Sszar" (Edict in play):** The Edict says only one champion per player may attack each turn. A Bout of single combat. Rask fights on the player's side.

# Ignara — The Living Pyre
*Elemental · Vanguard · Tier III · Chapter II · Blood and Ember*

> Status: PROPOSED lore (draft for Jerian's approval). Canon quotes are marked [canon].

## Who they are

"Ignara was born in a volcano. She considers everywhere else cold." [canon]

Ignara is a fire elemental warrior in cooling obsidian armour, tall and still and quietly smouldering. She does not shout and does not hurry. She simply stands in front of her friends, and anything that strikes her gets burned.

She is also always, always cold. Everywhere outside a volcano feels to her like a winter morning. She wraps herself in her own heat and wonders how anyone else manages.

## Before the Crownfall

Ignara was born in the heart of Mount Kharr, the greatest volcano of the Elemental realm, where fire spirits rise from the lava like bubbles in a pot. She grew in the deep heat for a hundred years before she ever left.

When she did, she found the outside world strange and chilly. She wandered the slopes, then the lowlands, guarding villages near the volcano from rockfalls and lava flows.

She became the Living Pyre: a guardian, a wall, a fire that never goes out.

## Why they answered the call

Ignara heard that the Crown above the Arena glows. She wants to know if it is hot.

That is the simple version, and she says it with a straight face. The real reason is quieter. In a hundred years of guarding the volcano's villages, she never once felt she belonged anywhere but inside the mountain. In the Crownfall, champions from every realm stand together, all of them far from home. She wanted to see if she could belong there.

## The Ascension

It was a cold, windy bout, and Ignara's armour had cooled almost to grey. Her banner was being driven back, step by step, across the frozen sand. A rival champion had broken through to the Crown Shards, and Ignara stepped into its path.

It struck her. Her armour cracked.

Then the cracks began to glow.

Heat roared up from inside her, through every split in the obsidian, until her armour was a lattice of black stone and bright orange flame. The rival champion leapt back, scorched. The air around Ignara rippled. For the first time since leaving the volcano, she felt warm.

"Burn brighter!" [canon]

She took another blow, and another, and every rival who struck her was burned for it. When the bout ended, she stood in the middle of the sand, her armour cracked and roaring, and smiled. It was the first time any of her friends had seen her smile.

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension

Ignara's Awakening came on the coldest night the Arena had ever seen. An Edict had turned the air to frost. Her friends were shivering, their hands too cold to hold their weapons. Even Emberling had shrunk to a flicker.

Ignara looked at them and understood. They were cold too. They always had been, in a way. They had just never told her.

She let go of her armour.

It fell away in glowing pieces, and what was left was a pillar of fire in the shape of a woman. The air around her burned. Frost turned to steam. Her friends gathered close, warming their hands, and rival champions who tried to reach them found only flame.

Every rival who attacked her, or who even came near enough to be attacked, was scorched.

When dawn came, Ignara stood alone in a circle of warm, dry sand. Every rival champion was gone.

"Only ash." [canon]

## Voice and temperament

Ignara is calm, stoic and dryly funny. She speaks rarely and briefly. Her greeting is a complaint: "Everywhere else is cold." [canon] She is fiercely protective and quietly lonely. She softens around small creatures, especially small fiery ones.

## Bonds and rivalries

- **Emberling** — The little living flame follows Ignara everywhere and thinks she is wonderful. Ignara pretends this is embarrassing. She has quietly decided that anything that tries to put Emberling out will have to go through her first.
- **Mirella** — Water to her fire. Mirella makes everything damp; Ignara makes everything dry. They argue constantly and fight beautifully side by side.
- **Pyrax** — The dragon-blooded warrior asked Ignara to teach her how to breathe fire. Ignara said fire is not taught. Pyrax has been trying anyway, near Ignara, for months. Ignara has started giving her small tips, grudgingly.

## Beneath the Arena (Set 2)

When the banners went down beneath the Arena, Ignara felt it immediately: heat. Deep under the Crownfall were the deep forges, great furnaces older than any realm she knew, still burning after untold ages.

For the first time in years, she did not feel cold at all.

Below, she became the **Heart of the Forge**, a Channeler burning at the heart of the deep forges with molten relics floating around her. Her ability, **Forgefire**, sends a burst of flame at a rival every time her banner casts a Tactic. At her strongest, she stands as a wall again, hard to get past.

## In the campaign

- **Trial 1 · Bout 6 — "The Wall of Fire":** A puzzle Bout teaching Block abilities. The rival must attack Ignara each turn. Win by letting the rival burn itself out against her.
- **Trial 2 · Bout 6 — "The Pyre's Test":** Ignara tests the player with an Elemental deck of fire blockers. The lesson: use Scorch and Tactics instead of attacks.
- **Trial 3 · Bout 6 — "The Coldest Night" (Edict in play):** The Edict gives each champion −1 Might. Ignara is the only champion on either side not affected. She fights on the player's side.

# Pyrax — Dragon-Blooded
*Elemental · Bruiser · Tier IV · Chapter II · Blood and Ember*

> Status: PROPOSED lore (draft for Jerian's approval). Canon quotes are marked [canon].

## Who they are

"Pyrax's grandmother was a dragon. Pyrax intends to be one too." [canon]

Pyrax is a dragon-blooded warrior with ember scales running up her arms, a wide grin and the confidence of someone who has decided exactly who she is going to be. She is strong, loud, quick to laugh and quicker to fight. She tries to breathe fire roughly twice a day.

She is not a dragon yet.

## Before the Crownfall

Pyrax grew up in the Elemental realm, in the ash-valleys at the foot of the fire mountains, where people with a little dragon in their blood have always lived.

Her grandmother was a dragon. Pyrax never met her. One day, long ago, her grandmother flew towards the sky and did not come back. Pyrax's family never knew why. They kept one of her scales on the mantelpiece, as big as a dinner plate and still warm.

Pyrax grew up staring at that scale. She decided, at about age six, that she would one day be a dragon herself.

## Why they answered the call

Pyrax heard about the Crownfall from a wandering bard who sang about the last dragon, Aurex, fighting in the Arena.

She answered a Sovereign's call for two reasons. The first is that the Crown is said to raise champions into more than they are, and Pyrax wants to be raised into a dragon. The second is quieter: she wants to find Aurex and ask him about her grandmother. She has a feeling, deep down, that he knows something. She may be right. Whether he will ever tell her is another matter.

## The Ascension

It was a fierce bout against a rival who mocked her. "Dragon-blooded?" the rival laughed. "You're a lizard with a temper." The crowd laughed too.

Pyrax did not laugh. She thought of the scale on her family's mantelpiece. She thought of her grandmother flying towards the sky.

Heat rose through her chest. Her scales spread up her arms, across her shoulders, down her side, until half her body glittered with ember-coloured scale. Two curved horns pushed up through her hair. A long tail swept out behind her, burning at the tip.

She grinned so wide her new teeth showed.

"The dragon wakes." [canon]

Fire burst from her in a wave and swept across every rival champion on the field. It was not a full dragon's breath, but it was real fire, and it was hers. The crowd stopped laughing. The rival stopped laughing too.

That night, Pyrax wrote a letter home. It said only: *Halfway.*

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension

Pyrax's Awakening came in a bout where Aurex was watching from the stands. She did not know he was there until halfway through. When she saw him, her heart nearly stopped.

She fought harder than she ever had. Every blow, every leap, every breath of fire, she did for the dragon watching her. And for her grandmother.

When the moment came, she felt it in her bones. They grew longer. Her scales covered her completely. Her face stretched into a snout, her claws sharpened, her whole body became a dragon in all but wings. On her back, two small hard bumps pushed up and ached.

The wings are coming. She could feel it.

She threw back her head and roared.

"Breathe fire!" [canon]

Real dragonfire poured out of her, a great gold-and-red torrent, scorching every rival and striking her opponent hard. When it was over, she looked up at the stands. Aurex was already gone.

"Scorched." [canon]

## Voice and temperament

Pyrax is bold, cheerful, competitive and full of energy. She is always testing her strength. Her greeting is "Grandmother would be proud." [canon] Underneath it all she is a little bit afraid that she will never be enough of a dragon to deserve the name.

## Bonds and rivalries

- **Aurex** — The last dragon. Pyrax has asked Aurex about her grandmother six times. Each time, he goes very quiet and walks away. She has decided to keep asking. He has not told her to stop.
- **Ignara** — Pyrax asked Ignara to teach her how to breathe fire. Ignara said fire is not taught. Pyrax practises near her anyway, and Ignara has started giving her small, grudging tips.
- **Fen** — The wolf hunter once tracked her for two days, thinking she was a monster. When he found her, she offered him half her lunch.

## Beneath the Arena (Set 2)

When the banners went down beneath the Arena, Pyrax did not walk down the stairs. She dug. She burrowed straight through stone with her claws, following the heat, until she broke into the deep forge-caverns, glowing and molten.

Below, she became the **Wyrm of the Deep Forge**, a Sapper burrowing through molten caverns with walls collapsing in her wake. Her ability, **Undermine**, breaks things: rivals' Relics, their hidden Schemes, and anything else they were counting on. At her strongest, she scorches the whole rival banner and brings down their plans at the same time.

## In the campaign

- **Trial 1 · Bout 7 — "Lizard With a Temper":** Pyrax's introduction. She challenges the player to a straight fight and taunts them throughout.
- **Trial 2 · Bout 7 — "The Dragon-Blooded Test":** Pyrax tests the player with an aggressive fire deck that Scorches every rival champion when her champions rise.
- **Trial 3 · Bout 7 — "Halfway" (Edict in play):** The last Bout before Aurex's Crowning. The Edict gives the player's champions +1 Might each time one of them Ascends. Pyrax fights on the player's side, and she wants Aurex to see.

# Aurex — The Last Dragon
*Wyrmblood · Ascendant, Bruiser · Tier V · Chapter II · Blood and Ember (The Crowning)*

> Status: PROPOSED lore (draft for Jerian's approval). Canon quotes are marked [canon].

## Who they are

"Every dragon died for the Crown. Aurex is here to find out whether it was worth it." [canon]

Aurex is a massive dragon warrior, scaled in deep red and gold, who walks on two legs and carries the weight of an entire people on his back. He is the last of the dragons.

He is not here for glory. He is here for an answer, and he is not sure he wants it.

## Before the Crownfall

Aurex comes from the Wyrmblood realm, the old home of the dragons: a land of high red peaks, great nests carved into mountains, and skies that were once full of wings.

What happened to the other dragons is not something Aurex speaks of easily. All that is known is that every dragon died for the Crown. How, and why, and whether they chose it, Aurex will not say. The stories told in other realms disagree. Some say the dragons flew to the Crown together, long ago, and never came back.

## Why they answered the call

Aurex's reason is canon, and he says it plainly to anyone who asks: he wants to find out whether the dragons' deaths were worth it. "The last dragon answers." [canon] That is how he greets a Sovereign, as if a call had reached him across ages.

He answers a Sovereign because no champion can face the Crown alone, not even a dragon. He hopes that if he gets close enough, it will tell him what his people's sacrifice bought. He fears it will tell him nothing at all.

## The Ascension

It was Aurex's first bout in the Arena, and the crowd had come to see a dragon. He gave them one. He tore through the rival banner with fire and claw, and still the Crown hung silent above him.

He stopped, in the middle of the sand, and looked up.

"Answer me," he said. The Crown's shards kept turning.

Then heat rose from within him, the old heat of the red peaks. Molten armour began to form over his folded wings, glowing like cooling metal, sealing them in shining plates. Fire leaked from every seam. He spread his armoured wings for the first time in ages, and they caught the air.

"My wings remember the sky." [canon]

He rose, just for a moment, above the Arena floor. The crowd gasped. Then he came down, fire pouring from every seam, and struck the next defender hard. It was not an answer. But it was something. The Crown had noticed him. Aurex was not sure whether to be glad.

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension

Aurex's Awakening is spoken about in every realm.

It happened during a Crowning. The rival had built a banner of champions meant to bring down a dragon. Aurex was wounded. His Sovereign was close to losing. And for a moment, high above the Arena, the Crown's shards seemed to slow, as if waiting.

Aurex stopped fighting. He looked up at the Crown and asked, aloud, in a voice that shook the stands, the question he had carried across all those years.

The Crown did not answer.

So Aurex answered for it.

His body grew and changed until he was a full celestial dragon, vast and blazing. The sky above the Arena turned red. A wall of flame rose around the sand. He opened his jaws.

"Let the world burn!" [canon]

Fire swept the field. Every weaker rival champion was gone in a moment. When the flames died down, Aurex stood alone on the scorched sand and looked at the Crown for a long time.

"Worth it? Perhaps." [canon]

## Voice and temperament

Aurex is grave, proud, weary and quietly sorrowful. He speaks slowly and deeply, and he does not waste words. He carries grief like armour. Even his victories sound like questions: "Worth it? Perhaps." [canon]

## Bonds and rivalries

- **Pyrax** — The dragon-blooded warrior whose grandmother was a dragon. She has asked Aurex about her grandmother six times. Each time he goes silent and walks away. He has never told her to stop asking.
- **Sol** — The other great legend of the first chapters. Aurex once said Sol "remembers too much and says too little." Sol said Aurex "says too much and remembers it all."
- **General Varr** — The old soldier who has held every line and never been given the Crown.

## Beneath the Arena (Set 2)

When something stirred beneath the Arena, Aurex went down alone, deep into the Guardians' lair. What he found there, and what he did, other champions only saw afterwards: the last dragon standing over a fallen Guardian.

Below, he became the **Bane of the Guardians**, an Ascendant and Hunter whose ability, **GUARDIAN'S BANE**, makes him deadly against Monsters, Guardians and anyone who has already Ascended. At his strongest, his fire sweeps the field as it once did above.

He has not said why he hunts the Guardians so fiercely.

## In the campaign

**Chapter II · The Crowning: Aurex.** The final Bout of Chapter II. Aurex plays a fire-and-fury deck of Scorch, big bruisers and Flight.

- **Contender:** Aurex plays the normal printing with a standard deck of fire champions. He Scorches every rival champion the moment he arrives.
- **Masterwork:** Aurex plays the Masterwork printing with a stronger deck and an Edict in its favour: "The Sky Remembers" (champions with Flight get +1 Might).
- **Omega:** Aurex plays the Omega printing, black and gold and signed. The hardest deck, and the Edict "Let the World Burn": at the start of each of Aurex's turns, Scorch 1 each rival champion.

# Pip — Demolitions Enthusiast
*Gearbound · Saboteur · Tier I · Chapter III · The Iron Covenant*
> Status: PROPOSED lore (draft for Jerian's approval). Canon quotes are marked [canon].

## Who they are
"Pip was told the Crownfall was a place of great explosions. Pip was not told correctly, so Pip brought his own." [canon]

Pip is a tiny goblin-like sapper with a satchel of fizzing bombs and no sense of danger at all. He talks about himself by name, laughs at every bang, and treats the Arena as the biggest, best workshop he has ever been allowed into. Most champions see him coming by the smoke. Most are too late anyway.

## Before the Crownfall
The Gearbound are "Machines of every age" [canon], but not every Gearbound is a machine. Some, like Pip, are the small folk who keep the machines running. [PROPOSED] Pip grew up in the Brasslands, a realm of foundries, rail-yards and half-buried engines from wars nobody remembers. His job was to clear old wreckage and make room for new builders. He was very good at it, and even better at making the blasts bigger than anyone asked for.

## Why they answered the call
[PROPOSED] A travelling tinker told Pip that the Crownfall was a floating kingdom where great powers clashed and the sky itself shook. Pip heard only one word: explosions. When a Sovereign's call reached the Brasslands, Pip packed before anyone had finished reading it. He does not want the Crown. He wants to see what happens when you light a fuse where the rules are made of magic.

## The Ascension
Pip's first Ascension came in the middle of a lost Bout. His Sovereign's line had been broken, and a shielded knight was marching on the Crown Shards. Pip had one bomb left. He looked at it. He looked at the knight. He shook his head.

"Too small," he muttered.

The Crown above the Arena turned, slow and bright, and a thread of gold light fell across the little sapper. His satchel split open and filled with something heavier. Brass goggles snapped over his eyes. A bandolier of fat, round bombs wrapped across his chest, and a smoking launcher unfolded on his back with a cheerful clank. Pip sniffed the smoke. He liked it.

He did not think. Pip rarely thinks first. He aimed the launcher at the sky, pulled the cord, and shouted "BIGGER BOOM!" [canon]

The volley arched over the Arena and came down in a ring of flame around the knight's whole company. When the smoke cleared, the Shards still stood. Pip was already reloading, giggling, asking if anyone had more fuses.

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension
Pip's Awakening is the story the Iron Covenant tells most often, mostly because nobody can tell it without laughing.

It was the final Bout of a long Trial. Pip's Sovereign was down to a single Shard. The rival line was a wall of iron and stone. Pip stood in front of it, bombs gone, launcher empty, and he looked up at the Crown and asked, very politely, for a ride.

The Crown answered. Gears fell out of the light like rain and assembled around him: tread wheels, a tiny turret, a crooked chimney, and, at the back, a bomb chute with a lever just the right height for a goblin. It was a ridiculous miniature war machine. It was perfect. Pip climbed into the seat and pulled the lever.

Bombs tumbled from the chute in a long, rolling line. The iron wall did not fall all at once. It fell one cheerful explosion at a time. When the last of it toppled, the Arena was silent except for Pip, hanging out of his little machine, waving at the stands.

"Pip wants to go again!" [canon]

## Voice and temperament
Pip is joy with a short fuse. He never sulks and never plans more than one blast ahead. He is not cruel; he simply cannot imagine anyone disliking explosions. "Pip brought explosives!" [canon]

## Bonds and rivalries
- **Professor Spark** [PROPOSED]: the closest thing Pip has to a teacher. Spark gives him parts; Pip gives Spark new reasons to be expelled from places.
- **HEX-3** [PROPOSED]: Pip is fascinated by the careful prototype and keeps asking what happened to prototypes one and two. HEX-3 does not enjoy these questions.
- **Torren** [PROPOSED]: the Titanborn shield-bearer has stood between Pip and his own blasts more than once. Pip calls him "the wall that says please."

## Beneath the Arena (Set 2)
Below the Arena, Pip becomes the **Tunnel Blaster**, a **Sapper** working the narrow mine shafts under the Crownfall. [PROPOSED] The vaults are full of relics the Guardians once held, and Pip has found a new favourite target. His **Tunnel Charge** is built to crack a rival's Relic loose and send it rattling down the stone. Down there, every boom echoes for a very long time. Pip considers this an improvement.

## In the campaign
- **Trial 1 · Bout 2: "Pip Brought His Own"** [PROPOSED]: a puzzle Bout. Pip's opening Scorch must be timed so it clears the rival line without wasting his only charge.
- **Trial 2 · Bout 5: "Special Delivery"** [PROPOSED]: Pip's own deck of cheap, fast Scorch effects tests whether the player can protect a line of low-Guard champions.
- **Trial 3 · Bout 1: "Bigger Boom"** [PROPOSED]: an Edict makes every Scorch deal 1 extra. Pip appears as an ally, and the gauntlet rewards anyone who lets him off the leash.

# Torren — Shield of the Deep
*Titanborn · Warden · Tier I · Chapter III · The Iron Covenant*
> Status: PROPOSED lore (draft for Jerian's approval). Canon quotes are marked [canon].

## Who they are
"The Titanborn say a shield is a promise. Torren has never broken one." [canon]

Torren is a stone-skinned shield-bearer who carries a slab of rock the size of a door and speaks only when it matters. He is not the strongest of the Titanborn, nor the loudest. He is the one who stays, and the champions behind him trust that he will still be standing when the dust settles.

## Before the Crownfall
The Titanborn are "Mountains wearing people" [canon], and their home is Titan's Deep, a realm of great caverns, underground rivers and halls carved into living rock. [PROPOSED] Torren grew up in a mining village at the edge of the Deep, where the tunnels shift and settle and sometimes collapse. His work was to stand in the dangerous places, holding up the roof with his shield while others ran to safety. Each time, he promised: "I will not move until you are clear." He never did.

## Why they answered the call
[PROPOSED] When a Sovereign's call echoed down into the Deep, Torren's village elders said a Titanborn should go, someone who would hold the line for the realm. They chose Torren, because he had never broken a promise. He made them one more promise before he left: that every champion who stood behind his shield would walk home. He means to keep it.

## The Ascension
The rival champions were throwing everything at Torren. Fire, steel, spells and stones crashed against his slab shield, and with every blow a new crack ran through the rock. Behind him, two smaller champions crouched low, too hurt to move.

The shield split down the middle.

Torren did not step aside. He pressed his broken shield together with both hands and said, under his breath, the old Titanborn words: a shield is a promise.

The Crown heard him. Light ran down into the cracks of the stone and filled them with gold. The two halves grew together and kept growing, broadening and rising until Torren held not a slab but a gate: a great carved door of stone, its surface covered in old Titanborn patterns, every line glowing with warm golden veins.

"Stone endures." [canon]

The rival's next strike broke against the gate and fell away. Behind it, his allies stood up again, sheltered and strong, and the Bout began to turn.

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension
Torren's Awakening came in a Bout where his whole banner was failing. One by one his allies fell, until only he and a single small champion still stood. The rival's strongest fighter stepped forward to finish it.

Torren set down his gate, knelt, and placed one hand on the Arena floor, as if listening to the mountain beneath it.

Then he rose, and the mountain rose with him.

The Crown's light lifted him until he seemed to be a whole peak made small. Crystal spires burst from his shoulders, clear and shining. His gate shield deepened into the colour of old granite, and in its center a single rune began to move, turning slowly, alive. Wherever its light touched, his allies were wrapped in stone-bright shields.

The rival's champion struck the living rune with everything it had. Torren did not move. Not a step. Not an inch.

When the Bout ended, the small champion behind him walked off the field without a scratch. Torren lowered his shield.

"Promise kept." [canon]

## Voice and temperament
Torren is calm, gentle and slow to speak. He says what he will do and then does it. His first words to any ally are always the same: "Stand behind me." [canon] He is fierce only when someone he protects is in danger.

## Bonds and rivalries
- **Borin Ironbelly** [PROPOSED]: a cousin from the forges of Titan's Deep. Borin talks; Torren listens. Borin forged the first rim for Torren's old slab shield and still claims the gate is "mostly my work."
- **Knuckle** [PROPOSED]: Torren and Knuckle grew up in different corners of the Deep. Knuckle thinks shields are for people who cannot hit hard enough. Torren has never answered. Knuckle has never got past him.

# Bramble — Sapling of the Greenwall
*Verdant · Warden · Tier I · Chapter IV · The Greenwall*
> Status: PROPOSED lore (draft for Jerian's approval). Canon quotes are marked [canon].

## Who they are
"The Greenwall forests sent their smallest guardian into the Crownfall. The forest does not understand crowns. It understands roots." [canon]

Bramble is a small wooden creature wearing moss like armour, with fists of knotted root and a heart as stubborn as an oak. He is young, earnest and very serious about his job, which is to stand in the way. He knows little about Crowns. He knows that if he holds on long enough, whatever is pushing him will give up.

## Before the Crownfall
The Verdant are the living forests, and "The forest mends its own." [canon] [PROPOSED] Bramble sprouted at the foot of the Greenwall, the vast wall of ancient trees that marks the edge of the Verdant realm. Every tree in the Greenwall has a guardian, and every guardian starts small. Bramble's tree was the youngest in the whole wall, a sapling no taller than a fence post. He spent his first seasons propping it up in storms and, once, bravely shouting at a very confused deer.

## Why they answered the call
The canon gives the reason, in the forest's own way: the Greenwall sent him. [PROPOSED] When a Sovereign's call reached the forest, the great trees argued for a season about who should go. The old ones were too deep-rooted to leave. The strong ones were needed at home. In the end, the forest chose the one guardian whose tree could spare him, the smallest of all. Bramble took it as the highest honour of his life. He still does. He intends to bring home something the forest can understand: proof that roots can hold anywhere, even in a floating kingdom.

## The Ascension
Bramble's first Ascension came in a Bout where a rival bruiser tried to walk straight through him. The bruiser was ten times his size. Bramble planted his feet, dug his root-toes into the Arena floor and refused to move.

The first blow knocked moss off his shoulders. The second cracked his bark. The third should have sent him flying. It did not, because by then Bramble's roots had found something deep beneath the sand and wrapped around it tight.

The Crown felt him hold on.

Light ran down Bramble's body and into his roots, and he began to grow. His mossy armour hardened into plates of bark, thick and layered. Green runes lit up across them, glowing softly. Along his shoulders, small white flowers opened all at once. When he finally took a step forward, roots trailed behind him like a cloak.

Bramble looked down at himself, astonished. Then he looked up, and his face split into a huge, delighted grin.

"I grew!" [canon]

The bruiser struck again. Bramble's roots slammed up out of the Arena floor to meet it, and this time it was the bruiser who went flying.

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension
Bramble's Awakening came in a Bout where the whole Greenwall seemed to be watching. A rival banner had broken every champion on his side. Bramble stood alone in front of his Sovereign's last Shard, small and scratched and still holding.

He closed his eyes and listened for the forest. Far away, he heard it answer.

The Crown's light fell over him like sunlight through a canopy. Bramble rose taller, his bark darkening into the deep brown of ancient wood, his limbs thickening like the trunk of a world-tree. Leaves lifted from the ground and began to circle him in a slow, green orbit. His eyes burned emerald.

He raised one fist and brought it down. Rootslam.

Enormous spectral roots burst from the Arena floor, glowing green, big enough to lift a castle. They rose all across the field and struck every rival champion at once, then sank gently back into the earth.

Bramble stood alone, leaves still circling. He touched the ground once, softly.

"The forest remembers." [canon]

## Voice and temperament
Bramble is brave, honest and a little bit clumsy. He takes everything literally and everything seriously. His first words to a new ally are always, "Roots first. Then questions." [canon]

## Bonds and rivalries
- **Gaia Prime** [PROPOSED]: the oldest tree titan of the Verdant treats Bramble like a favourite seedling. Bramble once asked her who built the Crownfall. She smiled and told him to grow taller first.
- **Thorn** [PROPOSED]: Bramble's prickly neighbour in the Greenwall. Thorn thinks Bramble is too friendly to strangers. Bramble thinks Thorn should try smiling. They protect each other fiercely anyway.
- **Hexie** [PROPOSED]: the young witch once turned Bramble into a toad by accident. He was a very sturdy toad. He has forgiven her. Mostly.

## Beneath the Arena (Set 2)
Below the Arena, Bramble's roots go where nothing else can. He becomes **The Rootbreaker**, a **Sapper**, splitting the flagstones of buried tunnels as he pushes through. [PROPOSED] Bramble did not mean to become a breaker of things. He was just trying to grow. His **Rootbreak** cracks open a rival's hidden Schemes and pries Relics loose. At its fullest, it shatters every Relic and Scheme the rival holds.

Hexie's first Ascension came in the middle of a spell going badly.

The Crown above, which may have been laughing, sent down a thread of light.

The hex hit her hat, and the hat ate it.

Hexie peeked out. Her hat had grown. It was wider, taller and much floppier, and two big round eyes had blinked open near the brim. They looked down at her. They looked very unimpressed. Hexie stared at her hat. Then at her staff. Then she burst out laughing.

Her staff complained. Around her, a swarm of glowing hexes appeared, drifting and blinking like fireflies on a summer night.

She had become a chaos witch, and she was having the best time of her life.

The cauldron swooped across the field. The hexes flew wherever she pointed, and wherever they landed, rival champions shrank, wobbled and stumbled. One very large one turned briefly into something green and surprised.

When it was over, Hexie leaned out of her cauldron and waved at the crowd.

"Ribbit. Ha!" [canon]

## Voice and temperament
Hexie is mischievous, bold and good-hearted. She jokes in the middle of danger and apologises in the middle of victories. Her favourite question is, "Who wants to be a toad?" [canon] Underneath the chaos is a young witch who wants to be taken seriously, and is learning that she already is.

# Grimm — The Soldier Who Stayed
*Hollow · Vanguard · Tier I · Chapter V · The Unremembered*

> Status: PROPOSED lore (draft for Jerian's approval). Canon quotes are marked [canon].

## Who they are

"His war ended three centuries ago. Nobody told Grimm." [canon]

Grimm is a skeleton in dented plate with a small ghost-light burning in his ribs, and he is the most dependable soldier in the Crownfall. He stands where he is told to stand. He holds what he is told to hold. He has been doing it for three hundred years, and the order to stop has simply never arrived.

He is not sad about this, or not in any way he would admit. He is polite, a little stiff, fond of rules and very bad at jokes he does not realise he is making. Ask him who he serves and he will salute. Ask him what war he is fighting and he will open his mouth to answer, and then close it again, and then salute anyway.

## Before the Crownfall

[PROPOSED] Grimm remembers the shape of his war but not its name. He remembers a gate he was ordered to hold, a column of comrades marching past him into the dark, and a captain who said, "Stay here until you are relieved." He remembers the cold. He remembers waiting. He does not remember the captain's face, the colour of the banner, or what was on the other side of the gate.

[PROPOSED] The Hollow realm is full of the half-remembered dead, and most of them drift. Grimm never drifted. When the gate he guarded crumbled, he stood among the stones. When the stones were buried, he stood in the dust. When travellers found him, centuries later, they were startled to be challenged for a password nobody had known for three hundred years. Most of them gave him a guess. He has never accepted one.

## Why they answered the call

[PROPOSED] A Sovereign's call is, to Grimm, the most wonderful thing that has happened in three centuries: an order. Someone needs a soldier. Someone has a banner. He does not much mind which Sovereign it is, as long as they speak clearly and point at something that needs holding.

[PROPOSED] There is a quieter reason underneath. Grimm has heard that the Crown can grant what a champion wants. He has never let himself want much. But if the Crown is real, it might know whether his war is over, and who won it, and whether anyone is still coming to relieve him. He would like, very much, to be relieved. He would also like, very much, not to be.

## The Ascension

[PROPOSED] It happened in his first season in the Arena, in a Bout everyone else had already given up on. His Sovereign's champions had fallen one by one, and only Grimm was left on the line, shield up, ribs cracked, the ghost-light in his chest flickering like a candle in a draught.

The rival's last blow came down. Grimm felt himself come apart, plate from bone, bone from light, the way he had always assumed it would end.

And then the Crown looked at him.

The pieces of his armour did not fall. They hung in the air, and a pale green seam of ghostfire ran along every crack, stitching them back together with spectral thread. Something heavy settled on his back. When he turned his head, he saw a banner rising from between his shoulder blades: tattered, faded, and blank. No colours. No crest. Just cloth that remembered being a flag.

The crowd went silent. Grimm looked down at his reforged hands, then back up at the rival who had been so certain.

"Still haven't died properly." [canon]

He held the line. His Sovereign won. Afterwards, the other champions asked what the banner meant. Grimm said it was standard issue.

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension

[PROPOSED] Grimm's Awakening came in the ruins of an old gate at the edge of the Arena's lower terraces, a gate so worn that the Arena keepers used it as a doorstop for the wind.

He was guarding it, of course. Nobody had asked him to.

The Bout went badly. His Sovereign's last champion fell, and the rival's legion poured toward the broken arch. Grimm planted his feet. His old sword snapped on the first blow. He reached for anything at all and closed his hand around a long jagged stone from the fallen gate itself.

The stone lit up in his grip. Green soulfire rolled from his ribs to his shoulders to his skull, and the stone became a blade: a shard of the fallen gate, sharp as an order given in a clear voice. Grimm did not grow taller. He simply stopped being something that could be knocked down. The rival champions struck him and struck him again, and every time they found him still standing there, wreathed in green flame, polite and patient and immovable.

When it was done, the field was quiet. Grimm lowered the gate-shard and looked at the empty arch behind him, the one nobody had needed him to hold.

"Another war ended." [canon]

He said it the way other people say *goodnight*. Then he went back to his post.

## Voice and temperament

Grimm speaks like a soldier giving a report: short, clear, faintly weary, always dutiful.

- "Still haven't died properly." [canon]

[PROPOSED] The "Again" is the whole of Grimm. He is not complaining. He is keeping count.

Whether Grimm once served under that erased name is not something either of them will say, and perhaps not something either of them knows.

Ask Dreadmouth which it would prefer and it will say, "Hungry…" [canon], which is not an answer.

## The Ascension

[PROPOSED] It happened in a Bout against a champion of iron and steam, a great clanking thing from the Iron Covenant that had never lost a fight. Dreadmouth bit it and broke a tooth. Then another. The crowd laughed. A jaw that cannot bite is not much of a jaw.

Dreadmouth sat back on the sand, mouth open, ghost-light dim. For a moment it looked almost puzzled, as if no one had ever said no to it before.

Then the Crown looked down.

Pale bone grew across its hide in overlapping plates, like armour that had been waiting to be asked. Its jaw split, and behind the first set of teeth a second jaw unfolded, row on row, newer and sharper. Deep in its throat a fire kindled: not warm and orange, but cold and green, the ghostfire of the Hollow.

Dreadmouth looked at the iron champion. The iron champion, for the first time in its life, took a step back.

"MORE TEETH." [canon]

The crowd stopped laughing. It was over very quickly after that.

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension

[PROPOSED] Dreadmouth Awakened in the Gullet itself, the valley of old teeth in the Hollow realm, where a strange Edict had carried a whole Bout. The arena floor became the valley. The valley was littered with the ancient teeth of everything Dreadmouth had ever bitten.

The rival Sovereign's legion advanced. Dreadmouth opened its mouth to meet them.

And the valley answered.

Every tooth in the Gullet rose from the earth at once: thousands of them, white and grey and yellow with age, sweeping up into the air like a flock of startled birds. They spiralled around Dreadmouth and came to rest along its jaws, its back, its sides, until the creature was no longer a beast with teeth but teeth shaped like a beast. A leviathan of the dead. An entire graveyard of teeth, swimming through the air.

The rival champions did not stand their ground. Nobody could have. When the dust settled, Dreadmouth drifted gently back down to the valley floor, ghostfire purring in its throat.

"Delicious." [canon]

And then, after a long pause, so quietly that only its Sovereign heard, it said, "Hungry…"

## Voice and temperament

Dreadmouth says almost nothing, and everything it says is about food.

- "Hungry…" [canon]
- "MORE TEETH." [canon]
- "Delicious." [canon]

[PROPOSED] The trailing dots in "Hungry…" are the most important part of its voice. It is not a roar. It is a sigh. Dreadmouth's hunger is less a threat than a sorrow it has stopped noticing.

## Bonds and rivalries

- **Grimm** [PROPOSED]. Grimm is the only champion Dreadmouth has never tried to eat. Grimm says this is because he is "all bone and no flavour." Dreadmouth seems to regard him as something like a fence post: reliable, familiar and not food. The two of them often stand watch together in silence.
- **Morrow** [PROPOSED]. Morrow raises the fallen to fight again. Dreadmouth makes the fallen very difficult to raise. They have argued, in their way, ever since Morrow reached for a soldier Dreadmouth had already finished with. Morrow calls it "a waste." Dreadmouth calls it "Delicious."
- **Noctara** [PROPOSED]. Something about Noctara makes Dreadmouth uneasy. It will not go near her. Some say a creature of endless hunger recognises something even emptier than itself. Others say it simply does not like how she smells. Noctara has never commented.

## Beneath the Arena (Set 2)

In Act II Dreadmouth becomes **Devourer of Guardians**, a **Hunter**. [canon scene] It is seen "rising from black water with a Guardian's armour still in its teeth."

[PROPOSED] Below the Arena lie flooded halls of black water where the Guardians sleep. Dreadmouth went down there first, before any Sovereign sent it, following the smell of something enormous. The champions who came after found it surfacing from a drowned vault with a piece of a Guardian's shell in its jaws. Nobody knows if the Guardian is still alive. Dreadmouth isn't saying, and it is still hungry.

Its ability, **Guardian Feast**, makes it far stronger when it fights big prey: Monsters, Guardians, and champions who have already Ascended or Awakened. At its fullest, every champion it defeats on your turn draws you a card, because a good meal sharpens the mind. **Ambush** lets it rise out of the dark before anyone sees it coming. Dreadmouth has stopped being a brute and become a hunter: it waits, it chooses, and it goes for the biggest thing in the room.

# Morrow — The Gravecaller
*Hollow · Summoner · Tier III · Chapter V · The Unremembered*

> Status: PROPOSED lore (draft for Jerian's approval). Canon quotes are marked [canon].

## Who they are

"Morrow believes death is a waste of good soldiers." [canon]

Morrow is a gaunt necromancer with a bone scythe and very firm opinions about efficiency. He does not think of himself as a villain, and in fairness he is not really one. He is an officer. He looks at a battlefield full of the fallen and sees a terrible waste of training, courage and perfectly good boots.

[PROPOSED] So he calls them back. Not cruelly: he asks first, in a voice like a cold wind across a parade ground, and those who rise do so because, somewhere deep down, they were not finished yet. Morrow's soldiers are loyal. Many of them are glad of the extra time. Some of them would like to rest now, please, and Morrow is still learning how to hear that.

## Before the Crownfall

[PROPOSED] Morrow was once a quartermaster in a Hollow army: the man who counted the shields, the spears, the boots, the soldiers. He was very good at it. He knew every name on every roll.

[PROPOSED] Then came a battle so bad that, at the end of it, his rolls were mostly crossed out. Morrow sat in the mud with his ledger and could not stand the sight of so many names with lines through them. So he walked out onto the field, knelt by the first fallen soldier he found, and read the name aloud. The soldier sat up.

[PROPOSED] He has been reading names aloud ever since. He keeps the old ledger still, tucked inside his robes, and his robes themselves are stitched from the banners of armies that no longer exist, because he cannot bear to see anything useful thrown away.

## Why they answered the call

[PROPOSED] Morrow answered the call because the Crownfall is the greatest battlefield there is, and therefore the greatest waste of soldiers. He believes that a Sovereign who wins the Crown will have the power to change how the realms fight, and he intends to be standing beside that Sovereign when they do. His dream is simple and slightly alarming: a world in which no soldier is ever lost, only reassigned.

[PROPOSED] There is something else, too. In the Hollow realm, there is one name Morrow cannot read aloud. It is not crossed out in his ledger. It is missing: a blank line where a name ought to be. He suspects it belongs to someone important. He suspects the Crown knows whose it is.

## The Ascension

[PROPOSED] It was a night Bout, under a low violet sky, and Morrow's Sovereign was losing badly. Fallen champions lay across the sand on both sides. Morrow walked among them with his scythe, reading names from his ledger, raising one after another, and still it was not enough.

He reached the last page. He had run out of names.

So he looked up at the banners hanging above the Arena: the old flags of a hundred forgotten armies, faded and moth-eaten. He reached out his hand.

The banners came down to him. They wrapped around his shoulders and stitched themselves into his robes, thread by thread, until he wore a cloak made of every army that had ever lost a war here. And from the seams, small ghostlights rose and began to orbit him, one for every soldier those banners had ever carried.

The fallen on the sand stirred. Then they stood. Then they turned to him and waited for orders.

Morrow lowered his scythe and smiled for the first time in a very long while.

"My legion grows." [canon]

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension

[PROPOSED] Morrow Awakened in the Buried Fields, an old graveyard at the edge of the Hollow realm where every stone is too worn to read. An Edict had carried the Bout there, and the dead beneath it were very, very old.

He knelt to read their names and found none. The stones were blank. The ledger had nothing to offer.

For the first time, Morrow did not command. He asked.

"Is anyone still here who would like to stand?"

One by one, small spectral candles lit along the tops of the stones. Then dozens. Then hundreds. They rose into the air and gathered above Morrow's head, settling into a ring of pale blue flame: a crown of candles, one for each soldier who said *yes*.

The Buried Fields rose with him. They fought with a calm and terrible discipline, and when the last rival champion fell, Morrow stood among them with the candles burning steady on his brow.

Then he did something he had never done before. He let them go.

"Rise, and rest." [canon]

The candles went out one by one, gently, like a village going to sleep.

## Voice and temperament

Morrow speaks like a tired officer with a long ledger: precise, a little stern, quietly kind.

- "Death is a waste of soldiers." [canon]
- "My legion grows." [canon]
- "Rise, and rest." [canon]

[PROPOSED] His win line is the whole of his arc. At first he only knew how to say *rise*. Learning to add *and rest* is what made him a legend.

## Bonds and rivalries

- **The Unwritten King** [PROPOSED]. The King also commands a legion of the dead, and Morrow cannot decide whether he admires him or disapproves of him. Morrow keeps careful rolls; the King's legion has no names at all. The blank line in Morrow's ledger troubles him every time the King walks past. He has never asked. The King has never offered.
- **Grimm** [PROPOSED]. Morrow's favourite soldier in the whole Arena, and the one he can never recruit, because Grimm insists he is already on duty. Morrow has offered him a promotion eleven times.
- **Dreadmouth** [PROPOSED]. A standing quarrel. Morrow raises the fallen; Dreadmouth eats them first. Morrow considers Dreadmouth the single most wasteful creature in all the realms. Dreadmouth does not consider Morrow at all.

## Beneath the Arena (Set 2)

In Act II Morrow becomes **Lord of the Buried Halls**, a **Binder**. [canon scene] He is found "raising the soldiers buried beneath the Arena from their stone tombs."

[PROPOSED] Beneath the Crownfall lie the Buried Halls: long galleries of stone tombs where the soldiers of old contests were laid to rest. Morrow went down to read their names and found that many of the tombs had been carved with no names at all. He has started giving them names himself, and raising them, and the Buried Halls are filling up with a quiet, well-organised army.

His ability, **Call of the Buried**, makes every token you control stronger: his Risen Soldiers stand taller and hold longer. At his fullest he can bring back two fallen champions at once, as long as they were only just Called and not too costly. As a **Binder**, Morrow is no longer just a raiser of the dead; he is the officer who holds a whole legion together.

When it's over, Morrow joins the banner: "Death is a waste of soldiers."

# The Unwritten King — Lord of the Forgotten
*Hollow · Ascendant / Summoner · Tier V · Chapter V · The Unremembered (the Crowning)*

> Status: PROPOSED lore (draft for Jerian's approval). Canon quotes are marked [canon].

## Who they are

"His name was erased from every record. He wants the Crown to write it back." [canon]

The Unwritten King is a skeletal monarch on a throne of shields. He is ancient, patient and terribly calm. He does not shout. He does not need to. Behind him, when he wishes it, stands a legion of the forgotten dead: soldiers with no banners, no faces anyone remembers, and no names.

He has been unwritten. Not killed; not defeated; *removed*. Every record that once held his name now holds a gap: a scraped patch on a stone, a torn line in a book, a blank in a song where the singer always pauses. The realms remember that there was a king. They do not remember who he was.

[MYSTERY] Who he was, what his name is, and why it was erased are not answered here, and must not be. The world disagrees about all three, and so do the champions.

## Before the Crownfall

[PROPOSED] The Hollow realm has many stories about the Unwritten King, and they contradict each other on purpose.

[PROPOSED] Some say he was a great and good king, erased by jealous enemies who could not bear his legend. Some say he was a terrible tyrant, erased by his own people so that no one would ever follow his example. Some say he erased himself, to hide something. Some say the Crown did it. A few very old Hollow folk say none of these, look at the ground, and change the subject.

[PROPOSED] The King himself does not confirm or deny any of them. When champions tell him a story about who he was, he listens to the whole thing courteously, and at the end says only, "Remember my name." [canon] Which is, of course, the one thing nobody can do.

[PROPOSED] What is known is this: there is a throne of shields in the deepest part of the Hollow, and every shield is blank. There was once a crest on each one. Every crest has been scraped away.

## Why they answered the call

[ESTABLISHED] The Unwritten King wants his erased name written back.

[PROPOSED] He believes the Crown can do it: that the Crown, which seems to watch and choose and remember, holds a record older and deeper than any book, and that a Sovereign who is Crowned could ask for one name to be returned to the world. He does not ask to rule again. He does not ask for his kingdom back. He asks only that his name be written down.

[PROPOSED] Some champions find this the saddest thing they have ever heard. Others find it the most frightening. Morrow, who has a blank line in his own ledger, wonders what will happen to everyone else's stories if one missing name is suddenly put back.

## The Ascension

[PROPOSED] It was the first time the Unwritten King took the field in the Crownfall. The crowd did not know his name. Of course they didn't. They only knew there was a skeleton on a throne of shields, and that the rival Sovereign's champions were laughing.

"Who are you?" one of them called. "Who would follow a king with no name?"

The King rose from his throne. He said nothing for a long moment. Then he lifted one bony hand, palm upward, as if asking a question of the empty air behind him.

The air answered. Shapes began to stand up out of nothing: pale soldiers in armour of every age, spears and swords and shields, rank on rank on rank. None of them had faces anyone could remember. None of them carried a banner. But every one of them stood straight, and every one of them looked at the King.

A spectral legion stood behind him, silent as snowfall.

"The forgotten answer." [canon]

The laughing stopped. Nobody asked his name again that day.

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension

[PROPOSED] His Awakening is the most retold moment in the Hollow realm, and nobody can retell it properly.

It happened at the very top of the Arena, in a Bout under the hanging Crown itself. The King's legion was broken; his Sovereign's last champions had fallen. The King stood alone before his throne of shields, and the Crown turned slowly above him, its shards catching the light.

And he remembered.

Everyone who was there agrees on that. His shoulders straightened. The hollow dark in his eye sockets filled with a steady, ancient light. He opened his mouth and spoke a name, his name, clearly and without hesitation.

Nobody in the crowd can say what it was. Some heard it. Some swear they heard it. Not one can repeat it. The scribes who wrote that day's records found, the next morning, a blank patch where the word should be.

But the dead heard it. Every grave in the Hollow, every forgotten field, every scraped-blank shield on his throne heard its king call. And they came.

"Legion — rise!" [canon]

The Arena filled with them, and the Bout was over.

## Voice and temperament

Grave, measured, utterly certain. He speaks as if every word were carved into stone, because his were once.

- "Remember my name." [canon]
- "The forgotten answer." [canon]
- "Write it down." [canon]

[PROPOSED] "Write it down" is not a boast. It is an instruction to whoever is watching. After every victory, he waits a moment, as if to see whether anyone will.

## Bonds and rivalries

- **Morrow** [PROPOSED]. The King and Morrow both command the dead, but differently: Morrow knows every soldier's name, the King's soldiers have none. Morrow carries a ledger with one blank line in it, and has never dared ask the King if it is his. The King has noticed the ledger. He has never asked to see it.
- **Grimm** [PROPOSED]. The day Grimm first saw him, Grimm saluted without knowing why. The King returned the salute exactly, with an old and formal gesture nobody in the Arena recognised. Neither has spoken of it since.
- **Crownless** [PROPOSED]. Crownless won the Crownfall and refused the Crown; the King would give anything to be granted one thing by it. The two regard each other with a cold, wary respect. Each seems to know something about the other that neither will say.

## Beneath the Arena (Set 2)

In Act II he becomes **Lord of the Buried Crown**, an **Ascendant** and a **Binder**. [canon scene] He is seen "enthroned in a buried throne room, an erased crown upon his head."

[PROPOSED] Deep under the Arena the champions find a throne room no record mentions, and a crown sitting on a skull on a throne of shields. The crown is real. It is also erased: wherever its gems and engravings should be, there is only smooth blank metal. The King is already there, or perhaps he was always there. He does not explain the crown. Some champions think it proves he once ruled a kingdom here, beneath the Crownfall. Others think he took it from someone else. The King only says, "Write it down."

[MYSTERY] Whether this crown has anything to do with *the* Crown is not answered.

His ability, **BURIED LEGION**, summons Fallen Warriors on arrival and makes all his tokens stronger. At his fullest he can raise two fallen champions from the grave at once and summon more warriors besides. As a **Binder**, he no longer simply calls the dead. He holds them together, rank after silent rank.

# Nyx — The Quiet Hand
*Umbral · Assassin · Tier I · Chapter VI · The Court of Night*

> Status: PROPOSED lore (draft for Jerian's approval). Canon quotes are marked [canon].

## Who they are

"Nyx stole a star once. She gave it back. Mostly." [canon]

Nyx is a hooded thief with two crooked daggers and a grin you can only ever catch the end of. She is quick, light-footed and almost impossible to pin down. She does not like being looked at. She likes even less being *caught* being looked at, which is why she is very rarely where you last saw her.

[PROPOSED] She is a thief, not a villain. She takes things because they are interesting, or because someone told her she couldn't, and she usually gives them back, eventually, in a slightly different place than she found them, with a small piece missing. Nobody in the Umbral realm has ever been able to work out what she does with the small pieces.

## Before the Crownfall

[PROPOSED] Nyx grew up in the alleys of the Umbral realm, a twilight country where the sun never fully rises and shadows have opinions. She learned early that a girl who keeps to the shadows can go anywhere, and that the shadows will hide you, if you are polite to them.

[PROPOSED] The star is the story everyone tells. One night, on a dare, Nyx climbed the tallest tower in the Umbral realm, reached up into the sky and took a star down. It was small and warm and hummed in her hands. For one night the whole Umbral sky had a hole in it, and Queen Vespera's court, which exists only when no one is looking, became very nervous indeed, because a star is a thing that is always looking.

[PROPOSED] Nyx put it back the next night. Mostly. A tiny chip of starlight stayed behind, and she still has it. Some say it is why her eyes glow violet when she Ascends. Nyx says it is none of your business.

## Why they answered the call

[PROPOSED] The Crown is the greatest treasure in all the realms, and it is hanging in the open air above an Arena, guarded by everyone at once. To a thief, that is not a warning. It is an invitation.

[PROPOSED] Nyx does not want to *keep* the Crown. She is very clear about that. She just wants to be the one who touched it. She wants to hold it for a moment, feel it hum the way the star did, and put it back, mostly. Every Sovereign who calls her knows this, and calls her anyway, because there is no one quicker.

## The Ascension

[PROPOSED] It happened in a Bout no one remembers well, because Nyx made sure no one was watching closely.

She slipped behind a rival's great armoured champion, daggers raised, and found herself in a patch of bright light, out in the open, every eye in the Arena on her. Nowhere to hide. The champion turned. The crowd held its breath.

Nyx did the only thing she could think of. She asked the shadows for help.

They came, as they always did, but this time they did not just hide her. They poured down over her shoulders and settled into her cloak, and her cloak began to drip, slowly, with living shadow, rippling like dark water. Beneath her hood two violet lights opened: her eyes, glowing with the colour of the star she had once held.

The armoured champion swung at where she was. She was not there.

When she reappeared, behind it, she was smiling. Her voice was barely a whisper.

"Quieter. Sharper." [canon]

Nobody saw what happened next. That was the point.

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension

[PROPOSED] Nyx Awakened on the night she finally reached for the Crown.

It was a Bout at the very top of the Arena, the Crown hanging close enough to touch. Her Sovereign was nearly beaten. The rival's champions stood between Nyx and the edge. And Nyx thought: *I could just take it. Right now. Just for a moment.*

She ran. And as she ran, she stopped being one girl.

Behind her came a trail of afterimages, a dozen Nyxes, then twenty, each a step behind the last, each fading like breath on glass. Half of her was shadow; half of her was girl; and nobody could tell which half was real. Her crooked daggers lengthened in her hands into blades carved from night itself, cold and dark and dotted with tiny stars.

She went through the rival champions like a rumour through a crowd. By the time they turned to strike, every afterimage was gone, and so was she.

She never touched the Crown. At the last moment she let her fingers brush the air beside it, laughed, and dropped back to the sand. The Bout was already won.

"Gone before they knew." [canon]

## Voice and temperament

Quick, cheeky, quietly proud. She talks in whispers because whispering is a habit, not because she is shy.

- "You didn't see me." [canon]
- "Quieter. Sharper." [canon]
- "Gone before they knew." [canon]

[PROPOSED] Nyx never raises her voice. If you ever hear Nyx shout, something has gone very wrong.

## Bonds and rivalries

- **Shade** [PROPOSED]. Shade is the only other figure in the Court of Night who can move as quietly as Nyx, and the two of them have an ongoing contest about it. Nyx has stolen Shade's mask exactly once. She gave it back very quickly, and has never told anyone what she saw when she looked behind it.
- **Queen Vespera** [PROPOSED]. Vespera has never forgiven Nyx for the night the Umbral sky had a hole in it. Nyx is not officially welcome in the Court of Night. She attends anyway, whenever no one is looking, which is when the court exists.
- **Grimm** [PROPOSED]. Nyx steals Grimm's helmet as a running joke. Grimm reports it missing every time. He has never once raised his voice about it, and Nyx has decided he is the nicest person in the Crownfall.

## Beneath the Arena (Set 2)

In Act II Nyx becomes **Vault Thief**, a **Rogue**. [canon scene] She is seen "slipping between treasure piles in a Guardian's lair, a stolen relic in hand."

[PROPOSED] When rumours reached the surface that the Guardians beneath the Arena sleep on hoards of ancient Relics, Nyx was gone before anyone could stop her. She has been down there ever since, slipping between treasure piles while the Guardians breathe slowly in the dark. She always takes one thing. She always leaves something small in its place: a button, a pebble, a single chip of starlight.

Her ability, **Light Fingers**, lets her take a Relic from a rival champion when she attacks and wear it herself. At her fullest, every champion she defeats on your turn makes your rival lose a card at random, because Nyx never leaves a room without checking its pockets. **Ambush** is with her always; **Flight** comes when she Ascends, the shadows lifting her over walls.

# Shade — The Mask With No Face
*Umbral · Assassin · Tier II · Chapter VI · The Court of Night*

> Status: PROPOSED lore (draft for Jerian's approval). Canon quotes are marked [canon].

## Who they are

"Shade has worn many faces. The mask is the only one that stayed." [canon]

Shade is a masked assassin with a red-lacquered blade, and nobody knows who is behind the mask. Possibly nobody does. Shade moves without sound, speaks without warmth and chooses targets the way a judge chooses a verdict: carefully, finally, and without apology.

[PROPOSED] In the Arena, Shade's "marks" and "executions" are a duellist's terms for defeating a champion in a Bout: champions who fall in the Crownfall return to their Sovereigns, and the game is about pride, not graves. But Shade speaks of them with a seriousness that makes even veterans uneasy.

## Before the Crownfall

[PROPOSED] In the Umbral realm, there was once a guild of masked duellists who served the Court of Night: the Faces. Each wore a different mask, and each mask came with a name, a voice, a history. A Face could become someone else entirely by changing masks. The best of them changed often.

[PROPOSED] Shade was the best of them. Shade wore the Smiling Face, the Weeping Face, the Face of the Old Man, the Face of the Young Prince, a hundred faces more, and lived each life fully while it lasted. Somewhere along the way, the face Shade was born with was set down, and forgotten, and lost.

[PROPOSED] Then came a night when the Faces were disbanded. Nobody in the Court will say why. Every mask was burned except one: a plain, smooth, blank mask that none of the Faces had ever worn, because it did not belong to anyone. Shade picked it up. It stayed.

## Why they answered the call

[PROPOSED] Shade does not explain. "A mark has been chosen" [canon] is the closest thing to an answer anyone has received.

[PROPOSED] The guess most often made in the Court of Night is that Shade wants a face back. Not one of the hundred masks: their own face, the first one, the one that was set down and lost. The Crown is said to grant what a champion truly wants. Perhaps it could return a face that even its owner has forgotten.

[PROPOSED] Another guess, quieter: that Shade wants to see what the Crown looks like when it is not being looked at. Some of the Court of Night believe the Crown, like their own court, is different when no one is watching. Shade intends to find out.

## The Ascension

[PROPOSED] Shade's Ascension happened in silence. Of course it did.

It was a Bout in the Court of Night's mirror hall, a long chamber walled with tall black mirrors. Shade's rival was a champion who could take anyone's appearance, and the rival had chosen to look like Shade: same mask, same red blade, same stillness. The crowd could not tell them apart.

The two masks faced each other. Shade looked into the copy, and for the first time in a very long time, wondered what was really underneath.

A sound broke the silence: a small, sharp *crack*.

A thin line had opened down the middle of Shade's mask, and from it spilled a crimson glow, warm and steady, like an ember under ash. Not a face. Not yet. But something. Something real.

The copy's mask stayed whole. It had nothing underneath at all. The crowd could tell them apart now.

Shade raised the red blade.

"The mask cracks. Good." [canon]

The copy did not last long after that.

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension

[PROPOSED] Shade Awakened during the hardest Bout in the Court of Night's memory: a duel against all of the old masks at once.

An Edict had called up the Faces, every one of the hundred lives Shade had once worn, rising from the burned guild hall as shadows wearing their own masks. The Smiling Face. The Weeping Face. The Old Man. The Young Prince. All of them reaching for Shade, all of them saying, *come back, be us again.*

Shade did not fight them one at a time. Shade simply let go.

The cloak unravelled into ribbons. The ribbons became knives. The hood fell back, and there was nobody inside it: an empty hood, and floating before it, the cracked mask, glowing crimson from within. Shade had become a wraith of ribbons and knives that answered to one face only, the one that had stayed.

The hundred old masks fell one by one to the ribbons. None of them was Shade anymore. None of them ever had been.

When the hall was silent, the mask turned toward the rival Sovereign.

"Executed." [canon]

## Voice and temperament

Cold, clipped, final. Shade says as little as possible and means all of it.

- "A mark has been chosen." [canon]
- "The mask cracks. Good." [canon]
- "Executed." [canon]

[PROPOSED] The "Good." is the only warmth Shade ever shows. Every crack in the mask is a step toward something real, and Shade welcomes every one.

Shade has never thanked her, never punished her, and never let her out of sight since. It might be a rivalry. It might be the closest thing Shade has to a friend.

# Queen Vespera — Monarch of the Umbral Court
*Umbral · Summoner · Tier IV · Chapter VI · The Court of Night*

> Status: PROPOSED lore (draft for Jerian's approval). Canon quotes are marked [canon].

## Who they are

"Vespera rules a court that exists only when no one is looking." [canon]

Queen Vespera is a pale shadow monarch who walks on invisible stairs. She is elegant, cool, perfectly mannered and very, very hard to see properly. Look straight at her and she is a woman in a dark gown with a thin silver crown. Look away, and from the corner of your eye you will glimpse a whole glittering court around her: courtiers bowing, ministers whispering, dancers turning in silence. Look back, and they are gone.

[PROPOSED] Her court is real. It simply only exists when nobody is watching it. Vespera has ruled it for longer than anyone can say, and she has learned to rule the way a moon rules the tides: quietly, constantly and from a distance.

## Before the Crownfall

[PROPOSED] The Umbral realm is a twilight country, and in its very centre stands a palace that most travellers say is empty. They walk its long halls and find dust, moonlight and an echo. What they do not notice is the court that pauses, mid-step, every time they turn a corner, and resumes the moment they have passed.

[PROPOSED] That is the Umbral Court, and Vespera is its monarch. Her courtiers are shades: graceful, quiet, loyal beings who can only move unseen. They carry her messages, keep her secrets and fight her wars, and nobody has ever proved any of them exist.

[PROPOSED] Vespera's greatest fear is being *watched*. A court that exists only when no one is looking can be undone by a single steady gaze. That is why she never forgave Nyx for stealing a star from the Umbral sky: a star is a thing that is always looking, and the night it was missing, her court could breathe freely for the first time in centuries. The night it was put back, mostly, her court froze again.

[PROPOSED] Vespera also remembers an empire that once stood beside her own: Noctara's. She does not speak of what became of it. She bows very low when Noctara passes, and does not raise her head until Noctara is gone.

## Why they answered the call

[PROPOSED] The Crown is watching. Everyone in the Crownfall knows it: "The Crown is watching." To Vespera, that is the most important sentence ever spoken. If the Crown is always looking, then a court like hers can never truly exist beneath it.

[PROPOSED] So Vespera came to the Crownfall to win the right to ask the Crown one thing: to look away. Only for a moment. Only long enough for the Umbral Court to step into the light, for every hidden courtier to be seen at last, and remain.

[PROPOSED] Some say she wants something larger and darker: a court that rules everywhere, whenever no one is watching, which is most of the time. Vespera has never denied this. She considers it impolite to deny things.

## The Ascension

[PROPOSED] Vespera Ascended in her own throne room, during the first Bout the Arena ever held in the Umbral realm.

The rival Sovereign's champions marched into the palace expecting an empty hall and a single pale queen. They kept their eyes fixed on her, every one of them, because they had been warned: do not look away from Vespera.

Vespera smiled. She lifted the long dark train of her gown and let it fall behind her down the invisible stairs.

And her train did not stop falling.

It became a river, a long rippling river of shadow, and in the river were her courtiers: dozens of them, then hundreds, rising out of the dark water of her gown in silks and masks, bowing, gliding, gathering around the rival champions. The rivals were looking at the queen. They could not look at the court. That was the whole trick.

The courtiers drew their thin dark blades and waited for her word. Vespera tilted her head, pleased.

"My courtiers grow bold." [canon]

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension

[PROPOSED] Vespera Awakened on the night of an eclipse over the Arena, the only night in memory when even the Crown seemed to dim.

An Edict had called the Bout into darkness, and for one long minute the whole Crownfall was in shadow. Nobody could see anything. Nobody, for that minute, was looking.

So Vespera's court did not have to hide.

From the dark behind her rose a throne: tall and black and carved from night, set with no jewels, because it needed none. Her thin silver crown went dark at the centre and blazed bright at the rim, until it was a perfect eclipse, a ring of fire around a circle of nothing, resting on her brow. Every courtier she had ever summoned stood up out of the dark, all at once, seen and unafraid, filling every seat of the Arena that the crowd could not see in the eclipse.

For one minute, the Umbral Court was real in front of everyone.

Vespera raised her hand.

"Rise, my court!" [canon]

When the eclipse passed, the rival Sovereign's champions were beaten, and the Arena was empty again. The crowd blinked. They were never sure what they had seen.

## Voice and temperament

Regal, unhurried, faintly amused. She speaks like someone who has never had to raise her voice to be obeyed.

- "Court is in session." [canon]
- "My courtiers grow bold." [canon]
- "Dismissed." [canon]

[PROPOSED] "Dismissed" is how Vespera ends every Bout and every conversation. Nobody has ever dismissed *her*.

## Bonds and rivalries

- **Noctara** [PROPOSED]. Vespera bows to no one except Noctara, and she bows very low. Whether that is loyalty, fear, grief or something else, Vespera does not say. Noctara accepts the bow without comment. Some in the Court of Night whisper that Vespera's court was once a corner of Noctara's empire, the only corner that was not looking when it disappeared. Vespera dismisses anyone who repeats it.
- **Nyx** [PROPOSED]. Officially banished from the Court of Night. Unofficially, Nyx attends every gathering, because she is very good at going places when no one is looking, and so is the court. Vespera pretends not to notice her. This is the most respect Vespera has ever shown a thief.
- **Solenne Ash** [PROPOSED]. Solenne has sworn to burn away every shadow in the Crownfall. Vespera finds this charming, in the way a cat finds a candle charming. She has invited Solenne to court. Solenne has not yet worked out whether that was a threat.

## Beneath the Arena (Set 2)

In Act II Vespera becomes **Empress of the Underhalls**, a **Binder**. [canon scene] She is seen "holding court in a vast underground throne room of shadow."

[PROPOSED] Beneath the Arena, far from the Crown's gaze, Vespera found what she had always wanted: a place where no one was looking. The Underhalls are vast, dark and silent, and her court can walk there freely. She has made herself Empress of them, and her shadow throne room grows larger every night as more courtiers dare to stay.

Her ability, **Court of Shadows**, summons Court Shades and makes every token you control stronger, and as she Ascends they grow tougher too, gaining Guard as well as Might. As a **Binder**, Vespera no longer only calls her courtiers. She holds an entire court together, bound to her by loyalty and darkness.

# Noctara — Empress of Nothing
*Umbral · Ascendant / Arcanist · Tier V · Chapter VI · The Court of Night (the Crowning)*

> Status: PROPOSED lore (draft for Jerian's approval). Canon quotes are marked [canon].

## Who they are

"Noctara ruled an empire until it disappeared. She is not sure she did not do it." [canon]

Noctara is an empress robed in the space between stars. Where light should fall on her gown, it falls *through*, into a deep and endless dark scattered with tiny pinpricks of distant light. She is calm, still, and utterly sure of herself in every way but one.

She does not know what happened to her empire.

[MYSTERY] What happened, what the Nothing is, and whether Noctara caused it are not answered here, and must not be. She does not know. The Arena does not say. Champions argue about it. That is all.

## Before the Crownfall

[PROPOSED] Once there was an empire in the Umbral realm, the greatest it had ever known. It had cities of black glass and towers that reached the stars, and its empress was Noctara.

[PROPOSED] Then, one night, it was gone. Not conquered. Not burned. Not ruined. *Gone*, as if it had never been, and in its place lay a vast, quiet emptiness that the Umbral folk call the Nothing. Noctara stood alone at the edge of it, in her robe of starless night, and could not remember what had happened in the moment before.

[PROPOSED] The stories disagree. Some say her empire was swallowed by something from outside the realms, and Noctara alone survived. Some say she reached for a power too large and it took everything she loved. Some say Dreadmouth ate it, which Noctara has never confirmed or denied. Some say she simply made a wish she should not have. Queen Vespera, who was there, says nothing at all.

[PROPOSED] Noctara has spent every day since trying to answer one question: *did I do it?* She is not sure she wants to know.

## Why they answered the call

[PROPOSED] Noctara has never said why she came to the Crownfall. Champions guess.

[PROPOSED] Some think she wants the Crown to bring her empire back. Some think she wants the Crown to tell her the truth of what she did. Some think she wants the Crown itself, because a power that seems to watch and choose and defend itself is the only thing in all the realms that might be as large as the Nothing. And a few, quietly, think she has come to make sure no one else ever makes the same mistake she did, whatever it was.

[PROPOSED] Her own words do not help: "All things end in me." [canon] Whether that is a threat, a warning or a confession, she does not explain.

## The Ascension

[PROPOSED] Noctara Ascended in her first Bout in the Arena, before a crowd that had come to see whether the rumours were true.

She did nothing at first. She simply stood on the sand, still as a statue, while the rival Sovereign's champions charged. The crowd waited for her to move.

Then a small dark circle appeared in the air beside her: no bigger than a coin, perfectly round, perfectly black. Then another. Then a dozen. They drifted around her slowly, like moons around a planet, and where they passed, light simply went out.

Her thin crown darkened at its centre until it was an eclipse, a circle of nothing with a burning ring around it. Her eyes did not change. Her voice did not rise.

The rival champions slowed. Then stopped. The tiny voids hung in the air between them and the empress, and nobody wanted to walk into one.

Noctara tilted her head, as if listening to something far away.

"The void deepens." [canon]

The crowd went home very quietly that night.

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension

[PROPOSED] Noctara Awakened on the one night she allowed herself to remember the edge of the Nothing.

The Bout had gone badly. Her Sovereign's champions were beaten; the rival legion was closing in. Noctara closed her eyes and thought of the place where her empire used to be.

And she opened.

There is no gentler way to say it. Noctara became a doorway. Not a door with a frame and a handle, but a shape of an empress filled with the deep endless dark of the space between stars, and through her, anyone who dared to look could see *nothing at all*. The sand of the Arena darkened under her feet. The walls dimmed. The sky above the Crownfall went black and starless, until the whole battlefield seemed to float in the void.

The rival champions did not fall. They faded, gently, one by one, back to their Sovereign's banner, unmade from the Bout as if they had never entered it.

Noctara stood alone in the darkness and spoke two words, softly, almost sadly.

"Be unmade." [canon]

When the light returned, she was simply an empress again. She was not smiling.

## Voice and temperament

Low, slow, absolute. Noctara speaks like the end of a story, and she knows it.

- "All things end in me." [canon]
- "The void deepens." [canon]
- "Nothing remains." [canon]

[PROPOSED] Her lines all sound like certainty. Her lore is all doubt. That gap is the character: an empress who sounds as though she knows everything, and who does not know the one thing that matters.

## Bonds and rivalries

- **Queen Vespera** [PROPOSED]. Vespera bows lower to Noctara than to anyone. Noctara has never asked her why. Perhaps she is afraid of the answer, because Vespera was there when the empire disappeared, and Vespera has never told her what she saw.
- **Dreadmouth** [PROPOSED]. Dreadmouth will not go near Noctara. Some say it ate her empire; some say it is the only creature that knows what really did. Noctara watches it with something close to curiosity. Neither has ever explained.
- **Sol** [PROPOSED]. "Before anything, there was Sol. Sol would like it back that way." [canon] Noctara says "All things end in me." The First Light and the Empress of Nothing stand at either end of every story, and each seems to regard the other as a puzzle that must not be solved. They are never placed on the same side.

## Beneath the Arena (Set 2)

In Act II Noctara becomes **Shadow Beneath the Crown**, an **Ascendant** and a **Seer**. [canon scene] She is seen beneath "a black hole opening in the cavern ceiling directly beneath the Crown."

[PROPOSED] Far beneath the Arena, in a great cavern lying directly under the hanging Crown, a circle of darkness has begun to open in the ceiling. Noctara was found standing beneath it, looking up. She says she did not make it. She says she does not know who did. She says it has been opening for a long time. Whether it is the Nothing, or something like it, or something else entirely, nobody can say, and Noctara has stopped guessing out loud.

[MYSTERY] Whether this darkness is connected to the Crown, the Nothing or Noctara herself is not answered.

As a **Seer**, Noctara now sees what others hide. Her ability, **ECLIPSE SIGHT**, makes your Schemes cheaper to spring, and on arrival she breaks one of your rival's face-down Schemes. *Voltborn · Ranger · Tier I · Chapter VII · Storm and Rift*

Kira grew up in a Voltborn storm-town of copper roofs and lightning rods, where the sky was something you harvested. One night a bolt split the oldest rod in town down the middle. Everyone else saw a ruined rod. Kira saw a barrel, a grip and a coil. By morning she had her first pistol.

## Why they answered the call

Kira wants to rebuild the Crown. [canon reason]

She has studied the shards through a homemade scope and filled a notebook with sketches of how they might fit together. She is fairly sure she is wrong about most of it. She is completely sure she is not wrong about all of it.

## The Ascension

Kira's first true fight in the Arena went badly. Her rival's champion was armoured, patient and far too strong. Her single pistol fired until the coil glowed red, and then it stopped.

She ducked behind a broken pillar and did what she always does. She took things apart.

Her coat had a spare coil sewn into the lining "for emergencies." She tore it free. She had the dead pistol in one hand and the coil in the other, and above her, through a gap in the pillar, the Crown's shards turned slowly in the sky.

The air went still. A thin thread of light ran down from the shards and touched her hands.

When she looked again, there were two pistols, both humming, both clearly hers. The coil hung from her coat, glowing like a captured storm.

Kira grinned and stood up from behind the pillar.

"Double the volts!" [canon]

The armoured champion did not get to be patient much longer. Afterwards, Kira insisted she had built the second pistol herself. The Crown, as usual, said nothing to correct her.

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension

It happened in a storm. The Arena had changed its terms that day, and the clouds had come down low and black over the Crownfall. Every other champion took cover.

Kira climbed the tallest tower on the Arena's rim.

For weeks she had been building little brass orbs meant to catch a lightning strike and hold it. None had ever worked. She set them in a ring around her anyway and raised both pistols to the sky.

The lightning came. It struck the tower, then the orbs, then her. For one bright moment she could not see.

When the light faded, the orbs were floating. They circled her slowly, crackling, each one full of storm. She fired once across the Arena, and the shot left a trail of lightning hanging in the rain behind it like a crack in the sky.

Far below, a rival Sovereign's banner fell. Kira blew a little smoke from one barrel.

"Nobody out-sparks me." [canon]

Quick, cheerful and loud, with the confidence of someone who has never failed at anything big enough to stop her. She talks to her tools and names her batteries.

- "Charged and ready!" [canon]
- "Nobody out-sparks me." [canon]

## Bonds and rivalries

# Dax Meridian — The Gun Between Worlds
*Riftborn · Ranger · Tier II · Chapter VII · Storm and Rift*

> Status: PROPOSED lore (draft for Jerian's approval). Canon quotes are marked [canon].

## Who they are

"Dax has fought in forty-one realities. He lost in one, and he is still annoyed about it." [canon]

Dax Meridian is a dimensional gunslinger in a dusty long coat, with a revolver that has been fired under more skies than most people know exist. Nothing in the Crownfall surprises him much. He has seen forty-one versions of most things.

## Before the Crownfall

PROPOSED: Dax was born in a dusty little town in a reality he refuses to name. One afternoon a crack in the air opened across the main street, and Dax, being Dax, walked through it to see what was on the other side.

He never found the way back. Instead, he found reality after reality, each a little different. In some the sky was green; in one the rivers ran uphill. In nearly all of them someone needed a quick hand with a gun.

He won forty times. Then, in one reality, he lost. He will not say where, to whom or how. He only says it was "a fluke."

## Why they answered the call

PROPOSED: The Crownfall is where the realms touch. For a drifter like Dax, it is the closest thing to a crossroads he has ever found. He answered a Sovereign's call because, from here, he might finally find the one reality he lost in.

He also has a simpler reason. He would like to win in this reality too.

## The Ascension

PROPOSED: It was his first proper Bout in the Arena, and Dax was enjoying himself. His rival kept trying to hide behind a big, armoured champion. Dax kept bouncing shots off the stones around it.

Then a shot went wrong. It struck a pillar and should have flown off into the stands. Instead the air in front of it cracked open like glass, and the bullet vanished.

It came back out of a second crack, behind the armoured champion, and hit it squarely.

Dax looked at his revolver. Thin rift-lines were glowing along the barrel, faint and blue. He looked down at his coat. The inside lining showed a sky that wasn't this one: purple, with two moons.

He knew that sky. He had fought there once. He had won.

He laughed, spun the cylinder and raised the gun again. The crack in the air opened willingly this time.

"Forty-two realities, one gun." [canon]

The rival Sovereign's champions did not have anywhere left to hide.

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension

PROPOSED: Dax Awakened at the end of a long, ugly Bout, outnumbered and nearly out of shots.

Valkara's Sovereign looked up at her. The crowd looked up at her.

## Who they are

"Once, someone won the Crownfall. They refused the Crown. Nobody has explained why." [canon]

Crownless is a figure in broken regalia whose face is always hidden. He is the only champion of his own kind: no realm claims him, and his origin is simply his name. He says almost nothing. When a Sovereign calls him, his whole answer is a silence.

"…" [canon]

He is one of the eight legends the Crown has raised highest, and the last Crowning of Act I.

## Before the Crownfall

PROPOSED: Nobody knows who Crownless was before. The stories disagree, and he corrects none of them.

Some say he was a king of a realm that no longer exists. Some say he was a nobody, a stable-hand or a farmer's child, who fought through the whole Crownfall on stubbornness alone. The regalia he wears is broken, but it is real regalia. Whose it was, he does not say.

What every story agrees on is the ending. Long ago, someone won the Crownfall, and the Crown was theirs to take. They turned it down. The stories say that someone was Crownless.

## Why they answered the call

Nobody has explained why. [canon]

That is the truth, and the stories keep it. Champions in the Crownfall have theories, and they argue about them often:

- Valkara thinks there must have been a reason that would survive a fair hearing. She cannot decide what it was.
- Astryon thinks no one who could win would ever walk away, so something must have been wrong with the Crown. Or with the winning.
- Elysian thinks Crownless was protecting someone.
- Veyra Sol says nothing, which some take to mean she knows. She may not.

Crownless has listened to every one of these theories. He has agreed with none and denied none. He still answers a Sovereign's call, still fights in the Arena, still walks to the end of the Trials. He still does not take the Crown.

## The Ascension

PROPOSED: The rival Sovereign had built a perfect board. Every champion stood where the rules said it was strongest. Every Edict favoured them. By any rule of the Arena, the Bout was over.

Crownless walked out to meet them anyway, broken regalia catching the light.

A rival champion struck him across the chest. Where the blow landed, his armour cracked, and through the crack came light: a thin bright line of Crown-light, gold and white, the same light that hangs above the Arena.

Then another crack. And another. The light spread through his armour like frost across a window, and where it ran, he grew stronger.

The rival Sovereign's champions stepped back. In the stands, people were standing up.

Ascension is supposed to cost a champion time. Crownless did not seem to have heard.

"The rules bend." [canon]

He was ready again before the crowd had finished gasping. The perfect board did not stay perfect for long.

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension

PROPOSED: It is said that Crownless Awakened only once in front of a crowd. Those who were there disagree about what they saw.

The Bout was the final one of a long Trial. The Arena had piled Edict upon Edict against him, rule after rule after rule, until it seemed the Crownfall itself did not want him to win.

Crownless stood still in the middle of the field. Then the cracks in his armour began to widen, and the light inside grew so bright that his armour was no longer armour. It was the Crown's own power, standing in the shape of a person, wearing broken regalia like an old coat.

Around him, the Arena began to fracture. The stones split in lines of light. The air cracked like ice. The Edicts hung above the field in glowing script, and one by one, they broke.

He raised his hand.

"I refuse your rules." [canon]

When it was over, the rival Sovereign's banner was down. High above, the Crown's shards had stopped turning, all at once, as if watching. Crownless looked up at them for a long moment, and then he looked away.

"Still not taking it." [canon]

## Voice and temperament

Almost silent. When he speaks, it is short, calm and final. He is never cruel and never cheerful.

- "…" [canon]
- "The rules bend." [canon]
- "Still not taking it." [canon]

## Bonds and rivalries

- **Valkara** (PROPOSED): The case she cannot close. She has asked him to explain himself. He has not. She has not judged. Both of them seem to be waiting for something.
- **Astryon** (PROPOSED): Astryon has asked him to explain, and then to duel. Crownless declined both.
- **Veyra Sol** (PROPOSED): She watches him like quarry. He does not seem to mind. They have spoken once, alone. Neither will say what about.
- **Elysian** (PROPOSED): She offered to heal the cracks in his regalia. He said nothing, and the cracks remained.

## Beneath the Arena (Set 2)

**The Unbound · Ascendant.** In Act II, Crownless goes down further than anyone. His Set 2 card shows him standing in an empty vault deep beneath the Arena, the place where, the card says, the Crown once rested, refusing it still. What the vault was, when the Crown rested there and why it is empty now are left as mysteries. Crownless knows where it is. He has not said how.

His ability, **UNBOUND**, weakens every rival champion's Might the moment he arrives. Ascended, he readies at once, as he always has. Awakened, he lends his strength to every champion beside him, and Scorches the defender when he strikes.

## In the campaign: the Crowning of Act I

Chapter VIII is named after him, and every Bout in it leads toward him. After Elysian's last Bout, the Arena goes quiet. The announcer does not speak. A single figure in broken regalia walks to the centre of the field and waits.

- **The Crowning: Crownless (Contender).** The finale of Act I. Crownless plays his standard printing with a deck built to break the player's expectations: champions that Ascend and ready at once, and Ambush where the rules say no attack should come. Win, and the Crown's shards stop turning for a moment. Crownless looks at the player's Sovereign, and then up at the Crown, and says "Still not taking it." [canon]. Act I ends there. He cannot be challenged again until Act II is complete.
- **Masterwork Crowning.** Reopens after Act II. Crownless plays his **Masterwork** printing (from Set 1) with a stronger deck and a proposed Edict, *The Rules Bend*: once per turn, his first champion to Ascend ignores one rule of the player's choosing.
- **Omega Crowning.** Crownless plays his black-and-gold **Omega** printing with his hardest deck and a proposed Edict, *I Refuse Your Rules*: all Edicts in play are removed at the start of his turn, and none can be added. It is the hardest fight in Act I. Beating it grants the top prestige reward (a title and card back, wording to be approved). What, if anything, Crownless says after this defeat is deliberately left unwritten: it must never explain the refusal.
