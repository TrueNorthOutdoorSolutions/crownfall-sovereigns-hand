# Morrow — The Gravecaller
*Hollow · Summoner · Tier III · Chapter V · The Unremembered*

> Status: PROPOSED lore (draft for Jerian's approval). Canon quotes are marked [canon].

## Who they are

"Morrow believes death is a waste of good soldiers." [canon]

Morrow is a gaunt necromancer with a bone scythe and very firm opinions about efficiency. He does not think of himself as a villain, and in fairness he is not really one. He is an officer. He looks at a battlefield full of the fallen and sees a terrible waste of training, courage and perfectly good boots.

[PROPOSED] So he calls them back. Not cruelly: he asks first, in a voice like a cold wind across a parade ground, and those who rise do so because, somewhere deep down, they were not finished yet. Morrow's soldiers are loyal. Many of them are glad of the extra time. Some of them would like to rest now, please, and Morrow is still learning how to hear that.

## Before the Crownfall

[PROPOSED] Morrow was once a quartermaster in a Hollow army: the man who counted the shields, the spears, the boots, the soldiers. He was very good at it. He knew every name on every roll.

[PROPOSED] Then came a battle so bad that, at the end of it, his rolls were mostly crossed out. Morrow sat in the mud with his ledger and could not stand the sight of so many names with lines through them. So he walked out onto the field, knelt by the first fallen soldier he found, and read the name aloud. The soldier sat up.

[PROPOSED] He has been reading names aloud ever since. He keeps the old ledger still, tucked inside his robes, and his robes themselves are stitched from the banners of armies that no longer exist, because he cannot bear to see anything useful thrown away.

## Why they answered the call

[PROPOSED] Morrow answered the call because the Crownfall is the greatest battlefield there is, and therefore the greatest waste of soldiers. He believes that a Sovereign who wins the Crown will have the power to change how the realms fight, and he intends to be standing beside that Sovereign when they do. His dream is simple and slightly alarming: a world in which no soldier is ever lost, only reassigned.

[PROPOSED] There is something else, too. In the Hollow realm, there is one name Morrow cannot read aloud. It is not crossed out in his ledger. It is missing: a blank line where a name ought to be. He suspects it belongs to someone important. He suspects the Crown knows whose it is.

## The Ascension

[PROPOSED] It was a night Bout, under a low violet sky, and Morrow's Sovereign was losing badly. Fallen champions lay across the sand on both sides. Morrow walked among them with his scythe, reading names from his ledger, raising one after another, and still it was not enough.

He reached the last page. He had run out of names.

So he looked up at the banners hanging above the Arena: the old flags of a hundred forgotten armies, faded and moth-eaten. He reached out his hand.

The banners came down to him. They wrapped around his shoulders and stitched themselves into his robes, thread by thread, until he wore a cloak made of every army that had ever lost a war here. And from the seams, small ghostlights rose and began to orbit him, one for every soldier those banners had ever carried.

The fallen on the sand stirred. Then they stood. Then they turned to him and waited for orders.

Morrow lowered his scythe and smiled for the first time in a very long while.

"My legion grows." [canon]

## The Awakening — the Ascension beyond Ascension

[PROPOSED] Morrow Awakened in the Buried Fields, an old graveyard at the edge of the Hollow realm where every stone is too worn to read. An Edict had carried the Bout there, and the dead beneath it were very, very old.

He knelt to read their names and found none. The stones were blank. The ledger had nothing to offer.

For the first time, Morrow did not command. He asked.

"Is anyone still here who would like to stand?"

One by one, small spectral candles lit along the tops of the stones. Then dozens. Then hundreds. They rose into the air and gathered above Morrow's head, settling into a ring of pale blue flame: a crown of candles, one for each soldier who said *yes*.

The Buried Fields rose with him. They fought with a calm and terrible discipline, and when the last rival champion fell, Morrow stood among them with the candles burning steady on his brow.

Then he did something he had never done before. He let them go.

"Rise, and rest." [canon]

The candles went out one by one, gently, like a village going to sleep.

## Voice and temperament

Morrow speaks like a tired officer with a long ledger: precise, a little stern, quietly kind.

- "Death is a waste of soldiers." [canon]
- "My legion grows." [canon]
- "Rise, and rest." [canon]

[PROPOSED] His win line is the whole of his arc. At first he only knew how to say *rise*. Learning to add *and rest* is what made him a legend.

## Bonds and rivalries

- **The Unwritten King** [PROPOSED]. The King also commands a legion of the dead, and Morrow cannot decide whether he admires him or disapproves of him. Morrow keeps careful rolls; the King's legion has no names at all. The blank line in Morrow's ledger troubles him every time the King walks past. He has never asked. The King has never offered.
- **Grimm** [PROPOSED]. Morrow's favourite soldier in the whole Arena, and the one he can never recruit, because Grimm insists he is already on duty. Morrow has offered him a promotion eleven times.
- **Dreadmouth** [PROPOSED]. A standing quarrel. Morrow raises the fallen; Dreadmouth eats them first. Morrow considers Dreadmouth the single most wasteful creature in all the realms. Dreadmouth does not consider Morrow at all.

## Beneath the Arena (Set 2)

In Act II Morrow becomes **Lord of the Buried Halls**, a **Binder**. [canon scene] He is found "raising the soldiers buried beneath the Arena from their stone tombs."

[PROPOSED] Beneath the Crownfall lie the Buried Halls: long galleries of stone tombs where the soldiers of old contests were laid to rest. Morrow went down to read their names and found that many of the tombs had been carved with no names at all. He has started giving them names himself, and raising them, and the Buried Halls are filling up with a quiet, well-organised army.

His ability, **Call of the Buried**, makes every token you control stronger: his Risen Soldiers stand taller and hold longer. At his fullest he can bring back two fallen champions at once, as long as they were only just Called and not too costly. As a **Binder**, Morrow is no longer just a raiser of the dead; he is the officer who holds a whole legion together.

- **Act II hook — "The Unnamed Tombs"** [PROPOSED] In the Buried Halls, Morrow asks the player to help him name the soldiers before he raises them. Each Bout won adds a name to the ledger.
